Exam plan

The exam is closed book, you won't need theorems word for word, and there's no heavy matrix algebra. What it tests is recognizing the trick that makes a short proof go through. So study in this order:

  1. Learn the 18 tricks below. For each one, know the signal in a problem statement that calls for it.
  2. Drill recognition. Open a homework drill or a new drill, read only the statement, and name the trick(s) before writing anything. The solution opens with the trick numbers, so you can check yourself.
  3. Carry in a handful of facts (listed below). These are distributional facts the tricks need, not theorems to recite.
  4. Use chapters 1–3 further down only as reference when a trick feels unfamiliar.
Writing the proof

Cite results by content, not number: "since $\bA\bY$ and $\bB\bY$ are jointly normal with zero covariance, they are independent." Then state the trick in your first line ("Add and subtract $\E\bX$…", "Since $Q$ is unchanged when every $Y_i$ is shifted, take $\mu=0$…"). Graders reward a visible plan.

Deprioritized

Given "no complex matrix manipulations": partitioned inverses, the projection-subspace (B.3) derivation of restricted LS, the §3.7 updating formulas, and g-inverses are now marked reference. Skim them last.

The 18 tricks

Grouped by what they accomplish. Each lists the signal that calls for it, the move, and where it already appeared.

Rewriting the object

T1 Add and subtract the mean

Spot it: an expectation of a square or outer product centered somewhere other than the mean, such as $\E\|\bX-\ba\|^2$ or $\|\bY-\bX\bbeta\|^2$.

Move: $\bX-\ba=(\bX-\bmu)+(\bmu-\ba)$. Expand; the cross term has a factor $\E(\bX-\bmu)=\bzero$ (or is orthogonal, see T2).

Seen in: HW 1a.1, Thm 1.5 proof 1, why $\bhat$ minimizes RSS.

T2 Pythagoras: kill the cross term

Spot it: "show X minimizes", "show RSS splits", or comparing two fits.

Move: write $\bY-\bX\bbeta=(\bY-\bX\bhat)+\bX(\bhat-\bbeta)$. The cross term vanishes because residuals are orthogonal to the column space: $\bX'\be=\bzero$ (normal equations). For restricted LS the cross term vanishes because $\bA(\bhat_H-\bbeta)=\bzero$. Conclude $\|\cdot\|^2=\text{fixed}+\text{nonnegative}$.

Seen in: L6 minimization, L11 identities, Thm 3.5(iv) method 2, Gauss–Markov.

T3 Shift invariance: set μ = 0

Spot it: the statistic only involves differences $X_i-X_j$ or deviations $X_i-\bar X$.

Move: replace $X_i$ by $X_i-\mu$; the statistic is unchanged, so assume $\mu=0$ and every $\bmu'\bA\bmu$ or mean term disappears. In matrix form this is $\bA\bone=\bzero$.

Seen in: L2 successive differences, L2 equicorrelation, HW 1b.5, 2b.8.

T4 A scalar is its own trace

Spot it: expectation of a quadratic form $\bX'\bA\bX$.

Move: $\E\bX'\bA\bX=\E\tr(\bA\bX\bX')=\tr(\bA\,\E\bX\bX')=\tr(\bA\Sig)+\bmu'\bA\bmu$. With $\Sig=\sigma^2\bI$ this is $\sigma^2\tr\bA$, so you only need the diagonal of $\bA$.

Seen in: Thm 1.5, Thm 3.3 ($\E S^2$), HW 1b.5(b), Misc 1.3.

T5 Write it as a matrix times Y

Spot it: sample means, deviations, differences, estimators: anything linear in the data.

Move: $\bar Y=\tfrac1n\bone'\bY$, deviations $=\bC\bY$ with $\bC=\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ$, differences $=\bA\bY$, $\bhat=(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'\bY$, $\be=(\bI-\bP)\bY$. Then $\Var(\bA\bY)=\bA\Sig\bA'$ and $\Cov(\bA\bY,\bB\bY)=\bA\Sig\bB'$ do the work, and normality passes through.

Seen in: HW 1a.3, 2c.2, every Thm 3.5 part.

T6 Quadratic form as a squared norm

Spot it: you need independence or a distribution for $\bY'\bA\bY$ and $\bA$ is a projection ($\bA=\bA'=\bA^2$).

Move: $\bY'\bA\bY=\bY'\bA'\bA\bY=\|\bA\bY\|^2$. A quadratic form becomes a function of the linear vector $\bA\bY$, and T9 applies.

Seen in: L5 independence example, $S^2=\|\bC\bY\|^2/(n-1)$, $\mathrm{RSS}=\|(\bI-\bP)\bY\|^2$.

Getting a distribution

T7 Standardize first

Spot it: normal data with mean $\mu\bone$, $\bX\bbeta$, or covariance $\Sig\ne\bI$.

Move: $\bZ=(\bY-\bmu)/\sigma$ or $\bZ=\Sig^{-1/2}(\bY-\bmu)$, so $\bZ\sim N(\bzero,\bI)$. Check $\bA\bmu=\bzero$ so the form in $\bY$ equals $\sigma^2\bZ'\bA\bZ$.

Seen in: L5 $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2$, Thm 2.9, Thm 3.5(iv) ($\mathrm{RSS}=\beps'(\bI-\bP)\beps$).

T8 Idempotent ⇒ χ², rank = trace

Spot it: "show $Q\sim\chi^2$" or "find $c$ so that $cQ$ is χ²".

Move: write $Q=\bZ'\bA\bZ$ with $\bZ\sim N(\bzero,\bI)$, check $\bA^2=\bA$, read degrees of freedom off $\tr\bA$. Identities that make the check one line: $\bJ^2=n\bJ$, $\bC\bJ=\bzero$, $\bP\bX=\bX$. To rule out χ², find one entry where $\bA^2\ne\bA$.

Seen in: HW 2d.4, Misc 2.8, L5 examples, Thm 3.5(iv).

T9 Zero covariance ⇒ independence (normal only)

Spot it: "show U and V are independent" with normal data.

Move: express both as functions of linear forms $\bA\bY$, $\bB\bY$ (T5, T6). Compute $\bA\Sig\bB'$; if zero, the linear forms are independent, and so are any functions of them. Say "jointly normal" explicitly: it is the condition graders look for.

Seen in: L4 $\bar Y\perp Q$, HW 2c.2, Thm 3.5(iii), Misc 2.8.

T10 Factor the joint MGF

Spot it: independence must be shown "from first principles" or without Chapter 2 machinery; or you must identify a distribution.

Move: compute $\E e^{sU+\bt'\mathbf V}$, rearrange the exponent (often completing a square, using $\sum(t_i-\bar t)=0$), and split it into (function of $s$)·(function of $\bt$). For distributions, match a known MGF and invoke uniqueness.

Seen in: HW 2b.8, Thm 2.4 proof, Cramér–Wold.

T11 χ² subtraction by MGF

Spot it: $Q_1=Q+Q_2$ with $Q_1,Q_2$ known χ² and $Q\perp Q_2$.

Move: $M_{Q_1}=M_QM_{Q_2}$, so $M_Q=(1-2t)^{-(r_1-r_2)/2}$ and $Q\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$.

Seen in: L2 example, Thm 3.5(iv) method 2.

T12 Rotate with an orthogonal matrix

Spot it: iid $N(0,1)$ (or $N(0,\sigma^2\bI)$) data and a sum of squares minus something.

Move: $\bX=\bP\bZ$ with $\bP$ orthogonal is still $N(\bzero,\bI)$ and $\|\bX\|=\|\bZ\|$. Choose the first row $\bone'/\sqrt n$ (Helmert) so $X_1=\sqrt n\bar Z$, giving $\sum(Z_i-\bar Z)^2=\sum_{i\ge2}X_i^2$. Eigen-decomposition $\bA=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda\mathbf T'$ is the same idea.

Seen in: L3 $\bar Y\perp Q$, Thm 2.7 proof.

T13 Show normality via every linear combination

Spot it: "show this stacked vector is multivariate normal".

Move: for any $\ba$, $\ba'\bW$ is a sum of independent normals, hence normal (Cramér–Wold).

Seen in: HW Misc 2.9.

Optimizing and comparing

T14 Unbiased for all parameters ⇒ constraints

Spot it: "find the minimum-variance unbiased linear/quadratic estimator".

Move: unbiasedness for every $\bbeta$ (or $\theta,\sigma^2$) turns into equations: $\bX'\ba=\bc$, or $\bone'\ba=1$, or $\tr\bA=1,\ \bone'\bA\bone=0$. Then write $\ba=\ba_0+\mathbf d$ with $\ba_0$ the candidate; the constraint kills the cross term (T2) so $\|\ba\|^2=\|\ba_0\|^2+\|\mathbf d\|^2$.

Seen in: HW 3b.3, 3c.2, Gauss–Markov.

T15 Difference is a square, so ≥ 0

Spot it: "show $\Var\tilde\theta\ge\Var\hat\theta$", "deduce each diagonal is smaller".

Move: write the difference as $\sigma^2\mathbf M'\mathbf K\mathbf M$ with $\mathbf K\succ0$, or as $\Var(\text{orthogonal piece})$; a PSD matrix has nonnegative diagonal. For scalars, Cauchy–Schwarz: $(\sum x_i)^2\le n\sum x_i^2$.

Seen in: HW 3g.3, 3c.2, Gauss–Markov, ratio estimator drill.

T16 MSE = variance + bias²

Spot it: comparing estimators of $\sigma^2$, or any "smaller mean-squared error".

Move: if $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_m$, then $c\,\mathrm{RSS}$ has mean $cm\sigma^2$ and variance $2c^2m\sigma^4$. Plug in.

Seen in: HW 3c.1, MLE vs $S^2$.

Using structure

T17 Specialize the regression model

Spot it: a one-sample problem that "should" follow from a regression theorem, or a small design.

Move: take $\bX=\bone_n$ ($p=1$): $\bhat=\bar Y$, $\mathrm{RSS}=\sum(Y_i-\bar Y)^2$, $\bP=\tfrac1n\bJ$. For two-parameter problems write $\bX'\bX$ explicitly. With orthogonal or centered columns, $\bX'\bX$ is (block) diagonal, so estimates decouple: $\hat\beta_j=\bx_j'\bY/\bx_j'\bx_j$.

Seen in: HW 3d.1, 3c.2, 3a.4, 3e.2, 3b.1, 3b.4.

T18 Polarization and products of moments

Spot it: a covariance between two quadratic forms, or between a linear and a quadratic form.

Move: $\Cov(U,V)=\tfrac12[\Var(U+V)-\Var U-\Var V]$. For $\E[X_iX_jX_k]$ or $\E[X_iX_jX_kX_l]$ with independent mean-zero $X$'s, only matching indices survive: $\E X_i^3=\mu_3$, pairs give $\sigma^4$.

Seen in: HW Misc 1.5, drill 1.

Facts to carry in

Not theorems to recite, but the raw material the tricks run on. Know them cold.

χ²

$\chi^2_r=\sum_{i=1}^rZ_i^2$; mean $r$, variance $2r$, MGF $(1-2t)^{-r/2}$. Independent χ²'s add.

Normal

MGF $\exp(\bt'\bmu+\tfrac12\bt'\Sig\bt)$. $\bA\bY+\mathbf d$ is normal with mean $\bA\bmu+\mathbf d$, covariance $\bA\Sig\bA'$.

t and F

$Z/\sqrt{V/k}\sim t_k$ and $(U/m)/(V/k)\sim F_{m,k}$, numerator and denominator independent.

Quadratic forms

$\E\bX'\bA\bX=\tr(\bA\Sig)+\bmu'\bA\bmu$. Normal, mean zero: $\Var(\bZ'\bA\bZ)=2\sigma^4\tr\bA^2$.

Matrices that recur

$\bC=\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ$, $\bJ^2=n\bJ$, $\bC\bone=\bzero$, $\tr\bC=n-1$. $\bP=\bX(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'$, $\bP\bX=\bX$, $\tr\bP=p$.

Regression basics

$\bhat=(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'\bY$, $\Var\bhat=\sigma^2(\bX'\bX)^{-1}$, $\bX'\be=\bzero$, $S^2=\mathrm{RSS}/(n-p)$, $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$, $\bhat\perp S^2$.

Homework drills

Every assigned problem. Read the statement, name the trick, then open it: the first line of each solution is the trick, followed by the route the official solution takes.

1a.1 · Shift identityProve $\E[(\bX-\ba)(\bX-\ba)']=\Var\bX+(\E\bX-\ba)(\E\bX-\ba)'$ and $\E\|\bX-\ba\|^2=\sum\sigma_{ii}+\|\E\bX-\ba\|^2$.

Trick: T1

Insert $\pm\E\bX$, expand into four terms, use $\E[\bX-\E\bX]=\bzero$ for the cross terms. Second part: the same with $(\cdot)'(\cdot)$, or take the trace of the first.

1a.3 · Variance from increments$Y_1=X_1$, $Y_i=X_i-X_{i-1}$ independent with unit variance. Find $\Var\bX$.

Trick: T5

$\bX=\bA\bY$ with $\bA$ the lower-triangular matrix of ones, $\Var\bY=\bI$, so $\Var\bX=\bA\bA'$, whose $(i,j)$ entry is $\min(i,j)$.

1b.2 · Unequal variances$X_i$ independent, common mean $\mu$, variances $\sigma_i^2$. Show $\E\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2=n(n-1)\Var\bar X$.

Trick: T1

$\Var\bar X=\frac1{n^2}\sum\sigma_i^2$. Then $\E\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2=\E\sum X_i^2-n\E\bar X^2=n\mu^2+\sum\sigma_i^2-n(\Var\bar X+\mu^2)=n^2\Var\bar X-n\Var\bar X$.

1b.5 · Var S² and successive differences$X_i$ iid $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $Q=\frac1{2(n-1)}\sum(X_{i+1}-X_i)^2$. (a) $\Var S^2=2\sigma^4/(n-1)$; (b) $Q$ is unbiased; (c) efficiency of $Q$ relative to $S^2$ tends to $2/3$.

Trick: T3, T4, T5

(a) $(n-1)S^2=\bX'\bC\bX$, $\bC\boldsymbol\theta=\bzero$, Theorem 1.6 leaves $2\sigma^4\tr\bC^2=2\sigma^4(n-1)$. (b) $2(n-1)Q=\|\bA\bX\|^2$ with $\bA$ the differencing matrix; $\E=\sigma^2\tr(\bA'\bA)=2(n-1)\sigma^2$. (c) $\Var[2(n-1)Q]=2\sigma^4\tr((\bA'\bA)^2)=2\sigma^4(6n-8)$, so $\Var Q=\frac{\sigma^4(6n-8)}{2(n-1)^2}$ and the ratio $\frac{4n-4}{6n-8}\to\frac23$.

Misc 1.3 · Unbiased estimate of Var X̄Common mean, $\Cov(X_i,X_j)=0$ unless $|i-j|=1$. With $Q_1=\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2$ and $Q_2=\sum(X_i-X_{i+1})^2$, show $\E\big[\frac{3Q_1-Q_2}{n(n-3)}\big]=\Var\bar X$.

Trick: T4

$\E Q_1=\sum\sigma_{ii}-n\Var\bar X$, $\E Q_2=2\sum\sigma_{ii}-2\sum\sigma_{i,i+1}$, $\Var\bar X=\frac1{n^2}(\sum\sigma_{ii}+2\sum\sigma_{i,i+1})$. Eliminate $\sum\sigma_{ii}$ and $\sum\sigma_{i,i+1}$.

Misc 1.5 · Covariance of two quadratic forms$\bX\sim N(\bzero,\sigma^2\bI)$: show $\Cov(\bX'\bA\bX,\bX'\bB\bX)=2\sigma^4\tr(\bA\bB)$.

Trick: T18

$\Cov=\frac12[\Var(\bX'(\bA+\bB)\bX)-\Var(\bX'\bA\bX)-\Var(\bX'\bB\bX)]=\sigma^4[\tr(\bA+\bB)^2-\tr\bA^2-\tr\bB^2]=2\sigma^4\tr(\bA\bB)$.

2b.8 · Ȳ ⊥ deviations via the joint MGF$Y_i$ iid $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$. Show $\bar Y\perp\sum(Y_i-\bar Y)^2$ using the MGF of $(\bar Y,Y_1-\bar Y,\dots,Y_n-\bar Y)$.

Trick: T10, T3

$s\bar Y+\sum t_i(Y_i-\bar Y)=\sum(t_i-\bar t+s/n)Y_i$. Independence of the $Y_i$ gives a product of normal MGFs; since $\sum(t_i-\bar t)=0$ the exponent splits as $[s\mu+\frac{\sigma^2s^2}{2n}]+[\frac{\sigma^2}2\sum(t_i-\bar t)^2]$, a function of $s$ times a function of $\bt$.

2c.2 · Equicorrelated normal sample$\bY\sim N_n(\mu\bone,\sigma^2[(1-\rho)\bI+\rho\bJ])$. Show $\bar Y\perp\sum(Y_i-\bar Y)^2$.

Trick: T5, T9

$\Cov(\tfrac1n\bone'\bY,(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)\bY)=\tfrac1n\bone'\Sig(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)$. Since $\bone'\Sig=\sigma^2\lambda\bone'$ with $\lambda=1+(n-1)\rho$, this is a multiple of $\bone'(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)=\bzero'$. Independence for every $\rho$.

2d.4 · Cyclic differences and χ²$\bY\sim N_3(\bzero,\bI)$: show $\frac13[(Y_1-Y_2)^2+(Y_2-Y_3)^2+(Y_3-Y_1)^2]\sim\chi^2_2$. Does this extend to $n\gt3$?

Trick: T8

The form is $\bY'\bA\bY$ with $\bA=\bI-\frac13\bJ$: symmetric, idempotent, rank $2$. For $n\ge4$ the matrix $\bB=k(2\bI-\text{cyclic neighbours})$ has $(\bB^2)_{13}\ne0=\bB_{13}$, so no $k$ makes it idempotent.

Misc 2.8 · Three independent χ²$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI)$, $\bA,\bB$ symmetric idempotent, $\bA\bB=\bB\bA=\bzero$. Show $\bY'\bA\bY$, $\bY'\bB\bY$, $\bY'(\bI-\bA-\bB)\bY$ are independent χ².

Trick: T8, T9

Check $(\bI-\bA-\bB)^2=\bI-\bA-\bB$ and $\bA(\bI-\bA-\bB)=\bB(\bI-\bA-\bB)=\bzero$; degrees of freedom are the traces.

Misc 2.9 · Bivariate normal sample$(X_i,Y_i)$ iid bivariate normal, $\bW=(X_1,\dots,X_n,Y_1,\dots,Y_n)'$. (a) $\bW\sim N_{2n}$; (b) find $\bX\mid\bY$.

Trick: T13; conditional via T9

(a) Cramér–Wold: $\ba'\bW=\sum_i(a_iX_i+a_{n+i}Y_i)$, a sum of independent normals. (b) $\Sig_{12}=\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2\bI$, $\Sig_{22}=\sigma_2^2\bI$: $\ \bX\mid\bY\sim N_n\big(\mu_1\bone+\frac{\rho\sigma_1}{\sigma_2}(\bY-\mu_2\bone),\ \sigma_1^2(1-\rho^2)\bI\big)$.

3a.2 · Residuals sum to zeroWith an intercept, show $\sum(Y_i-\hat Y_i)=0$.

Trick: T2

$\bX'\hat\bY=\bX'\bX\bhat=\bX'\bY$, so $\bX'(\bY-\hat\bY)=\bzero$; the first row of $\bX'$ is $\bone'$.

3a.4 · Orthogonal quadratic design$\E Y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1x_i+\beta_2(3x_i^2-2)$, $x=(-1,0,1)$. Find the LSEs; show $\hat\beta_0,\hat\beta_1$ unchanged if $\beta_2=0$.

Trick: T17

$\bX'\bX=\mathrm{diag}(3,2,6)$: $\hat\beta_0=\bar Y$, $\hat\beta_1=\frac12(Y_3-Y_1)$, $\hat\beta_2=\frac16(Y_1-2Y_2+Y_3)$. Columns are orthogonal, so each estimate uses only its own column.

3b.1 · Straight line, uncorrelated estimatesFind $\hat\beta_0,\hat\beta_1$ and show they are uncorrelated iff $\bar x=0$.

Trick: T17

Invert the $2\times2$ $\bX'\bX$; $\Cov(\hat\beta_0,\hat\beta_1)=\sigma^2[(\bX'\bX)^{-1}]_{12}=-\sigma^2\bar x/S_{xx}$.

3b.3 · BLUE of a normal meanFind the minimum-variance linear unbiased estimate of $\theta$ from a $N(\theta,\sigma^2)$ sample.

Trick: T14

$\bX=\bone_n$; Gauss–Markov gives $\bar Y$. Direct proof: $\ba=\bone/n+\mathbf d$, $\mathbf d'\bone=0$, $\ba'\ba=\frac1n+\mathbf d'\mathbf d$.

3b.4 · Variance inflationCentered two-covariate model: show $\Var\hat\beta_1=\sigma^2/[\sum(x_{i1}-\bar x_1)^2(1-r^2)]$.

Trick: T17

$\bX'\bX$ is block diagonal $(n)\oplus\begin{pmatrix}S_{11}&S_{12}\\S_{12}&S_{22}\end{pmatrix}$; take the $(1,1)$ entry of the $2\times2$ inverse and use $r^2=S_{12}^2/(S_{11}S_{22})$.

3c.1 · A biased estimator beats S²$\bY\sim N_n(\bX\bbeta,\sigma^2\bI)$. Find $\Var S^2$; evaluate $\E(\bY'\bA_1\bY-\sigma^2)^2$ for $\bA_1=\frac{\bI-\bP}{n-p+2}$; show it beats $S^2$ in MSE.

Trick: T16

$\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_m$, $m=n-p$: $\Var S^2=2\sigma^4/m$. $\bY'\bA_1\bY=\mathrm{RSS}/(m+2)$ has bias $-2\sigma^2/(m+2)$ and variance $2m\sigma^4/(m+2)^2$, so MSE $=2\sigma^4/(m+2)\lt2\sigma^4/m$.

3c.2 · Best nonnegative quadratic unbiased estimator$Y_i$ iid mean $\theta$, variance $\sigma^2$. Find the nonnegative quadratic unbiased estimate of $\sigma^2$ with minimum variance.

Trick: T14, T15, T17

Official route: Theorem 3.4 with $\bX=\bone$ ($\bP=\frac1n\bJ$ has equal diagonal) gives $S^2$. Direct route: unbiased for all $\theta$ ⇔ $\tr\bA=1$, $\bone'\bA\bone=0$; nonnegative ⇒ $\bA\bone=\bzero$; minimize $(\mu_4-3\sigma^4)\sum a_{ii}^2+2\sigma^4\tr\bA^2$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, giving $\bA=\frac1{n-1}\bC$.

3d.1 · Ȳ ⊥ Q from Theorem 3.5Use Theorem 3.5 to show $\bar Y\perp Q=\sum(Y_i-\bar Y)^2$ and $Q/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$.

Trick: T17

Take $\bX=\bone_n$, $p=1$: $\bhat=\bar Y$, $\mathrm{RSS}=Q$. (iii) gives independence, (iv) the distribution.

3e.2 · Choosing an orthogonal polynomial$\E Y_i=\beta_0+\beta_1x_i+\beta_2\phi(x_i)$, $x=(-1,0,1)$, $\phi$ quadratic. Find $\phi$ making the columns orthogonal.

Trick: T17

$\phi(x)=a+bx+cx^2$: $\bx'\boldsymbol\phi=2b=0$ and $\bone'\boldsymbol\phi=3a+2c=0$, so $\phi(x)=d(3x^2-2)$, $d\ne0$.

3g.3 · Variance of the restricted estimatorProve $\Var\bhat_H=\sigma^2\{\bG-\bG\bA'\bK^{-1}\bA\bG\}$ with $\bG=(\bX'\bX)^{-1}$, $\bK=\bA\bG\bA'$, and deduce $\Var\hat\beta_{Hj}\le\Var\hat\beta_j$.

Trick: T5, T15

$\bhat_H=(\bI-\bG\bA'\bK^{-1}\bA)\bhat+\text{const}$, so $\Var\bhat_H=\sigma^2(\bI-\bG\bA'\bK^{-1}\bA)\bG(\bI-\bA'\bK^{-1}\bA\bG)$. Expand: the two cross terms each give $-\bL$ and the last gives $+\bL$, where $\bL=\bG\bA'\bK^{-1}\bA\bG$, since $\bA\bG\bA'=\bK$. Result $\sigma^2(\bG-\bL)$. $\bL=(\bA\bG)'\bK^{-1}(\bA\bG)\succeq0$, so its diagonal is nonnegative: $\Var\hat\beta_j-\Var\hat\beta_{Hj}=\sigma^2l_{jj}\ge0$.

New drills

Not from the homework. Same exercise: name the trick before opening.

1. Linear–quadratic covariance $X_i$ iid, mean 0, variance $\sigma^2$, third moment $\mu_3$. Show $\Cov(\bar X,\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2)=\frac{n-1}{n}\mu_3$.

Trick: T18, T3

Solution. $\Cov=\E[\bar X\,\bX'\bC\bX]=\frac1n\sum_{i,j,k}c_{jk}\E[X_iX_jX_k]$; only $i=j=k$ survives: $\frac1n\mu_3\tr\bC=\frac{n-1}n\mu_3$. Zero under normality, as Theorem 2.5 predicts.

2. When is a variance matrix PD $\bX=\bA\bY$ with $\Var\bY=\bI_n$, $\bA$ $n\times n$. Show $\Var\bX$ is PD iff $\bA$ is nonsingular.

Trick: T5

Solution. $\Var\bX=\bA\bA'$ by Theorem 1.3, and $\bc'\bA\bA'\bc=\|\bA'\bc\|^2$, which is $0$ for some $\bc\ne\bzero$ iff $\bA'$ is singular.

3. Joint law of Ȳ and one deviation $\bY\sim N_n(\mu\bone,\sigma^2\bI)$. Find the distribution of $(\bar Y,Y_1-\bar Y)'$.

Trick: T5, T9

Solution. $\bC$ has rows $\frac1n\bone'$ and $\mathbf e_1'-\frac1n\bone'$ (rank 2). Theorem 2.2: mean $(\mu,0)'$, covariance $\sigma^2\bC\bC'=\sigma^2\mathrm{diag}(\frac1n,1-\frac1n)$. Zero off-diagonal ⇒ independent (Theorem 2.4).

4. A χ² under equicorrelation $\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\Sig)$ with $\Sig=\sigma^2[(1-\rho)\bI+\rho\bJ]$, $0\le\rho\lt1$. Find $c$ such that $c\sum(Y_i-\bar Y)^2\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$.

Trick: T7, T8

Solution. $c\sum(Y_i-\bar Y)^2=\bY'(c\bC)\bY$. Since $\bC\bJ=\bzero$, $(c\bC)\Sig=c\sigma^2(1-\rho)\bC$. This is idempotent iff $c=1/[\sigma^2(1-\rho)]$, and then its rank is $\tr\bC=n-1$. Corollary to Theorem 2.8 ($\Sig\succ0$).

5. Fitted and residual sums of squares Normal regression: show $\hat\bY'\hat\bY\perp\mathrm{RSS}$ and give their distributions.

Trick: T6, T9, T8

Solution. $\hat\bY'\hat\bY=\bY'\bP\bY$, $\mathrm{RSS}=\bY'(\bI-\bP)\bY$, $\bP(\bI-\bP)=\bzero$. $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$ (Theorem 3.5). Under $\bbeta=\bzero$, $\bY'\bP\bY/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_p$ by Theorem 2.7; otherwise it is noncentral (textbook only). For independence with any $\bbeta$: $\hat\bY'\hat\bY=\|\bP\bY\|^2$, $\mathrm{RSS}=\|(\bI-\bP)\bY\|^2$, and $\Cov(\bP\bY,(\bI-\bP)\bY)=\sigma^2\bP(\bI-\bP)=\bzero$, so Theorem 2.5 applies.

6. Residual variances Show $\sum_i\Var e_i=(n-p)\sigma^2$ and $\Cov(\be,\hat\bY)=\bzero$.

Trick: T5, T4

Solution. $\Var\be=\sigma^2(\bI-\bP)$, trace $n-p$. $\Cov((\bI-\bP)\bY,\bP\bY)=\sigma^2(\bI-\bP)\bP=\bzero$.

7. Ratio estimator vs least squares $Y_i=\beta x_i+\varepsilon_i$. Show $\tilde\beta=\sum Y_i/\sum x_i$ is unbiased and compare with the LSE.

Trick: T15

Solution. $\Var\tilde\beta=n\sigma^2/(\sum x_i)^2$, $\Var\hat\beta=\sigma^2/\sum x_i^2$. Cauchy–Schwarz $(\sum x_i)^2\le n\sum x_i^2$ gives $\Var\tilde\beta\ge\Var\hat\beta$, equality iff all $x_i$ equal.

8. Dropping an orthogonal column Orthogonal columns. Show the RSS of the model without column $j$ exceeds the full RSS by $\hat\beta_j^2\|\bx^j\|^2$, and find its distribution under $\beta_j=0$ with normal errors.

Trick: T6, T8, T9

Solution. Subtract the two RSS formulas. Also $\hat\beta_j^2\|\bx^j\|^2=\bY'\bP_j\bY$ with $\bP_j=\bx^j(\bx^{j\prime}\bx^j)^{-1}\bx^{j\prime}$ rank 1; under $\beta_j=0$, $\bP_j\bX\bbeta=\bzero$, so it is $\sigma^2\chi^2_1$, independent of RSS since $\bP_j(\bI-\bP)=\bzero$.

9. Restricted estimator satisfies the restriction Show $\bA\bhat_H=\bc$, $\E\bhat_H=\bbeta$ when $H$ holds, and find the bias when it fails.

Trick: T5

Solution. $\bA\bhat_H=\bA\bhat+\bK\bK^{-1}(\bc-\bA\bhat)=\bc$. $\E\bhat_H=\bbeta+\bG\bA'\bK^{-1}(\bc-\bA\bbeta)$, which is $\bbeta$ iff $\bA\bbeta=\bc$.

10. Extra RSS from the restriction Show $\mathrm{RSS}_H-\mathrm{RSS}=(\bA\bhat-\bc)'\bK^{-1}(\bA\bhat-\bc)$.

Trick: T2

Solution. Identity (i): $\mathrm{RSS}_H-\mathrm{RSS}=\|\bX(\bhat-\bhat_H)\|^2=(\bhat-\bhat_H)'\bX'\bX(\bhat-\bhat_H)$. Substitute $\bhat-\bhat_H=\bG\bA'\bK^{-1}(\bA\bhat-\bc)$; then $\bK^{-1}\bA\bG\bX'\bX\bG\bA'\bK^{-1}=\bK^{-1}$.

11. GLS beats OLS $\Var\beps=\sigma^2\bV$. Show $\E\bbeta^*=\bbeta$, $\Var\bbeta^*=\sigma^2(\bX'\bV^{-1}\bX)^{-1}$, and $\Var(\bc'\bhat)\ge\Var(\bc'\bbeta^*)$.

Trick: Transform to the standard model, then T14

Solution. First two by Theorems 1.1 and 1.3. For the third, $\bc'\bhat$ is linear and unbiased in the transformed model $\bZ=\bB\bbeta+\boldsymbol\eta$, where Gauss–Markov says $\bc'\bbeta^*$ is BLUE.

12. MLE of σ² vs S² Under normality compare the MSE of $\hat\sigma^2_{\rm MLE}$ and $S^2$.

Trick: T16

Solution. $\nu=n-p$. $\mathrm{MSE}(S^2)=2\sigma^4/\nu$. $\hat\sigma^2$ has bias $-\frac pn\sigma^2$ and variance $\frac{2\nu}{n^2}\sigma^4$, so MSE $=\frac{\sigma^4}{n^2}(2\nu+p^2)$. Smaller than $S^2$ iff $p(\nu-2)\lt4\nu$, true for every $\nu$ when $p\le4$.

0Matrix toolkit

Almost every proof reduces to one of these facts.

Trace

$\tr(\bA\bB)=\tr(\bB\bA)$; trace is linear and commutes with $\E$; a scalar is its own trace, so $\bx'\bA\bx=\tr(\bA\bx\bx')$.

Symmetrizing

$\bX'\bA\bX=\bX'\big(\tfrac{\bA+\bA'}{2}\big)\bX$, so any quadratic form can use a symmetric matrix. L1

Square root

$\Sig=\bP\boldsymbol\Lambda\bP'$, $\Sig^{1/2}=\bP\boldsymbol\Lambda^{1/2}\bP'$ is symmetric with $(\Sig^{1/2})^2=\Sig$. L3

Rank of $\bX'\bX$

$\rank(\bX'\bX)=\rank\bX$. Full column rank ⇒ $\bX'\bX$ is positive definite and invertible.

Centering matrix

$\bC=\bI_n-\tfrac1n\bJ_n$: symmetric, idempotent, rank $n-1$, $\bC\bone=\bzero$, $\sum(Y_i-\bar Y)^2=\bY'\bC\bY$, $\bJ^2=n\bJ$.

PSD sums

$\bB'\bB\succeq0$; $\bK\succ0$ ⇒ $\bM'\bK\bM\succeq0$. Diagonal entries of a PSD matrix are $\ge0$.

Projections Appendix A.6L4

A square $\bP$ is idempotent if $\bP^2=\bP$; a symmetric idempotent matrix is a projection.

  • (A.6.1) $\bP$ symmetric: idempotent of rank $r$ ⇔ $r$ eigenvalues equal to 1 and $n-r$ equal to 0.
  • (A.6.2) Projection ⇒ $\tr\bP=\rank\bP$.
  • (A.6.3) $\bP$ idempotent ⇒ $\bI_n-\bP$ idempotent.
  • (A.6.4) Projections are PSD.
  • (A.6.5) $\bP_1,\bP_2$ projections with $\bP_1-\bP_2\succeq0$ ⇒ $\bP_1\bP_2=\bP_2\bP_1=\bP_2$ and $\bP_1-\bP_2$ is a projection.

  • (B.3.3) $W=\mathrm{Null}(\bM)\cap\Omega$ ⇒ $W^\perp\cap\Omega=\mathcal C(\bP_\Omega\bM')$.

L1Expectation and covariance operators

Definitions 1.1–1.3

$\E(\bZ)=(\E Z_{ij})$; $\Cov(\bX,\bY)=(\Cov(X_i,Y_j))$; $\Var(\bX)=\Cov(\bX,\bX)$.

Theorem 1.1 linearitycore

$\E(\bA\bZ\bB+\bC)=\bA\E(\bZ)\bB+\bC$. Corollary: $\E(\bA\bX+\bC)=\bA\E(\bX)+\bC$.

Theorem 1.2

If $\E\bX=\ba$, $\E\bY=\bb$: $\ \Cov(\bX,\bY)=\E[(\bX-\ba)(\bY-\bb)']=\E(\bX\bY')-\ba\bb'$. Corollary: $\Var(\bX)=\E(\bX\bX')-\ba\ba'$.

Theorem 1.3 bilinearitycore

$\Cov(\bA\bX,\bB\bY)=\bA\Cov(\bX,\bY)\bB'$. Corollary: $\Var(\bA\bX)=\bA\Var(\bX)\bA'$.

Proof

$\bA\bX-\E(\bA\bX)=\bA(\bX-\ba)$, so $\Cov(\bA\bX,\bB\bY)=\E[\bA(\bX-\ba)(\bY-\bb)'\bB']=\bA\Cov(\bX,\bY)\bB'$ by Theorem 1.1.

Theorem 1.4 variance matrices are PSDcore

$\Var(\bX)$ is non-negative definite. If no element of $\bX$ is a linear combination of the others (no $\ba\ne\bzero$, $b$ with $\ba'\bX=b$), then $\Var(\bX)$ is positive definite.

Proof (lecture)

For any $\bc$: $0\le\Var(\bc'\bX)=\bc'\Var(\bX)\bc$. Equality iff $\bc=\bzero$ or $\bc'\bX=b$ for some constant $b$; the second is ruled out, so $\bc'\Var(\bX)\bc>0$ for $\bc\ne\bzero$.

Shift identity HW 1a.1core $$\E[(\bX-\ba)(\bX-\ba)']=\Var(\bX)+(\E\bX-\ba)(\E\bX-\ba)',\qquad \E\|\bX-\ba\|^2=\textstyle\sum_i\sigma_{ii}+\|\E\bX-\ba\|^2.$$

Add and subtract $\E\bX$; cross terms vanish because $\E[\bX-\E\bX]=\bzero$.

L1–2Mean and variance of quadratic forms

Theorem 1.5 mean of a quadratic formcore

$\E\bX=\bmu$, $\Var\bX=\Sig$, $\bA$ symmetric:

$$\E(\bX'\bA\bX)=\tr(\bA\Sig)+\bmu'\bA\bmu.$$

Corollary: $\E[(\bX-\bb)'\bA(\bX-\bb)]=\tr(\bA\Sig)+(\bmu-\bb)'\bA(\bmu-\bb)$. If $\Sig=\sigma^2\bI_n$, then $\tr(\bA\Sig)=\sigma^2\tr\bA$.

Proof 1 (entrywise, lecture)

$\E[(\bX-\bmu)'\bA(\bX-\bmu)]=\E(\bX'\bA\bX)-\bmu'\bA\bmu$. Also $\E[(\bX-\bmu)'\bA(\bX-\bmu)]=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\sigma_{ij}=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\sigma_{ji}=\sum_i(\bA\Sig)_{ii}=\tr(\bA\Sig)$, using symmetry of $\Sig$.

Proof 2 (trace trick, lecture)

$\E(\bX'\bA\bX)=\E[\tr(\bA\bX\bX')]=\tr[\bA\E(\bX\bX')]=\tr[\bA(\Sig+\bmu\bmu')]=\tr(\bA\Sig)+\bmu'\bA\bmu$.

Lecture 2 examples core

Successive differences. $X_i$ iid $(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $Q=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(X_i-X_{i+1})^2$. $Q$ does not depend on $\mu$, so take $\mu=0$. $Q=\bX'\bA\bX$ with $\bA$ tridiagonal: diagonal $(1,2,\dots,2,1)$, off-diagonal $-1$. Then $\E Q=\sigma^2\tr\bA=(2n-2)\sigma^2$.

Equicorrelation. $\Var\bX=\sigma^2[(1-\rho)\bI+\rho\bJ]$, $Q=\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2=\bX'\bC\bX$. Since $\bC\bJ=\bzero$, $\bC\Sig=\sigma^2(1-\rho)\bC$, so $\E Q=\sigma^2(1-\rho)(n-1)$.

Trick used in both: if $Q$ is invariant to shifting all $X_i$ by $\mu$, set $\mu=0$ so the $\bmu'\bA\bmu$ term disappears (formally, $\bA\bone=\bzero$).

Theorem 1.6 variance of a quadratic formcore

$X_1,\dots,X_n$ independent, means $\theta_i$, common central moments $\mu_2,\mu_3,\mu_4$; $\bA$ symmetric, $\ba=$ diagonal of $\bA$:

$$\Var(\bX'\bA\bX)=(\mu_4-3\mu_2^2)\,\ba'\ba+2\mu_2^2\tr(\bA^2)+4\mu_2\,\boldsymbol\theta'\bA^2\boldsymbol\theta+4\mu_3\,\boldsymbol\theta'\bA\ba.$$

Normal case ($\mu_3=0$, $\mu_4=3\mu_2^2$), $\bZ\sim N(\bzero,\mu_2\bI)$: $\ \Var(\bZ'\bA\bZ)=2\mu_2^2\tr(\bA^2)$.

How HW 1b.5 uses it

For $(n-1)S^2=\bX'\bC\bX$ with $\boldsymbol\theta=\mu\bone$: $\bC\boldsymbol\theta=\bzero$ kills the last two terms, normality kills the first, and $\tr\bC^2=\tr\bC=n-1$. So $\Var S^2=2\sigma^4/(n-1)$. Always check whether $\bA\boldsymbol\theta=\bzero$ first.

Covariance of quadratic forms HW Misc 1.5

$\bX\sim N(\bzero,\sigma^2\bI)$: $\ \Cov(\bX'\bA\bX,\bX'\bB\bX)=2\sigma^4\tr(\bA\bB)$.

Proof: polarization, $\Cov(U,V)=\tfrac12[\Var(U+V)-\Var U-\Var V]$, then Theorem 1.6 on $\bA+\bB$, $\bA$, $\bB$.

L2Independence and moment generating functions

Independence

$\bX\perp\bY$ iff $f_{\bX,\bY}(\bx,\mathbf y)=f_{\bX}(\bx)f_{\bY}(\mathbf y)$ (or the CDFs factor) for all $\bx,\mathbf y$.

Propositions
  • $\bX\perp\bY$ ⇒ $\Cov(\bX,\bY)=\bzero$. Proof: the double integral of $xyf_X(x)f_Y(y)$ factors into $\E X\cdot\E Y$.
  • $\bX\perp\bY$ ⇒ $\E[g(\bX)h(\bY)]=\E g(\bX)\,\E h(\bY)$.
  • Uncorrelated does not imply independent: $X\sim U(-1,1)$, $Y=X^2$ has $\Cov(X,Y)=\E X^3-\E X\,\E X^2=0$.
MGF and characteristic function

$M_{\bX}(\bt)=\E e^{\bt'\bX}$, $\varphi_{\bX}(\bt)=\E e^{i\bt'\bX}$. The CF always exists and determines the distribution; the MGF determines it when it exists near $\bzero$.

Property 1.1

$\bX\perp\bY$ (same dimension) ⇒ $M_{\bX+\bY}(\bt)=M_{\bX}(\bt)M_{\bY}(\bt)$.

Property 1.2 MGF test for independencecore

$\bX\perp\bY$ ⇔ $M_{\bX,\bY}(\bt_1,\bt_2)=M_{\bX}(\bt_1)M_{\bY}(\bt_2)$, where $M_{\bX,\bY}(\bt_1,\bt_2)=\E e^{\bt_1'\bX+\bt_2'\bY}$.

χ² subtraction lemma Lecture 2, used in Thm 3.5core

$M_{\chi^2_r}(t)=(1-2t)^{-r/2}$ for $t\lt\tfrac12$. If $Q_1\sim\chi^2_{r_1}$, $Q_2\sim\chi^2_{r_2}$ ($r_1>r_2$), and $Q=Q_1-Q_2$ is independent of $Q_2$, then $Q\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$.

Proof: $M_{Q_1}(t)=\E e^{tQ+tQ_2}=\E e^{tQ}\,\E e^{tQ_2}$, so $\E e^{tQ}=(1-2t)^{-r_1/2}/(1-2t)^{-r_2/2}=(1-2t)^{-(r_1-r_2)/2}$.

L3Definitions

Definition 2.1 density

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$, $\Sig$ positive definite, if

$$f(\mathbf y)=k^{-1}\exp\!\left\{-\tfrac12(\mathbf y-\bmu)'\Sig^{-1}(\mathbf y-\bmu)\right\},\qquad k=(2\pi)^{n/2}\det(\Sig)^{1/2}.$$
Theorem 2.1

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ ⇒ $\E\bY=\bmu$, $\Var\bY=\Sig$.

Property 2.1 and 2.2 standardizing

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ ⇒ $\bZ=\Sig^{-1/2}(\bY-\bmu)\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$. Conversely $\bZ\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$ ⇒ $\Sig^{1/2}\bZ+\bmu\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$. Proof: Jacobian change of variables.

Definition 2.2 allows singular Σcore

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ if $\bY\overset d=\bA\bZ+\bmu$ with $\bZ\sim N_m(\bzero,\bI_m)$, $\bA$ $n\times m$, $\Sig=\bA\bA'$. If $\rank\Sig=m\lt n$, the distribution is singular. For $\Sig$ PD, Definitions 2.1 and 2.2 agree (one direction by Theorem 2.2 with $\bZ=\bA^{-1}(\bY-\bmu)$; the other by matching MGFs).

Proposition MGFcore $$M_{\bY}(\bt)=\exp\!\left(\bt'\bmu+\tfrac12\bt'\Sig\bt\right).$$
Proof (lecture)

$M_{\bZ}(\bt)=\prod_i\E e^{t_iZ_i}=\prod_ie^{t_i^2/2}=e^{\bt'\bt/2}$. Write $\bY=\Sig^{1/2}\bZ+\bmu$ and $\mathbf s=\Sig^{1/2}\bt$ (symmetric root): $\E e^{\bt'\bY}=\E e^{\mathbf s'\bZ+\bt'\bmu}=\exp(\tfrac12\mathbf s'\mathbf s+\bt'\bmu)=\exp(\tfrac12\bt'\Sig\bt+\bt'\bmu)$.

Theorem 2.2 linear transformationscore

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$, $\bC$ is $m\times n$ of rank $m$ ($m\le n$), $\mathbf d$ is $m\times1$:

$$\bC\bY+\mathbf d\sim N_m(\bC\bmu+\mathbf d,\ \bC\Sig\bC').$$

Proof: with $\mathbf s=\bC'\bt$, $\E e^{\bt'(\bC\bY+\mathbf d)}=e^{\bt'\mathbf d}M_{\bY}(\mathbf s)=\exp(\tfrac12\bt'\bC\Sig\bC'\bt+\bt'\bC\bmu+\bt'\mathbf d)$. Full row rank keeps $\bC\Sig\bC'$ positive definite.

  • $\bZ\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI)$, $\bA$ nonsingular ⇒ $\bA\bZ+\bmu\sim N_n(\bmu,\bA\bA')$.
  • $\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\sigma^2\bI)$, $\bP$ orthogonal ⇒ $\bP\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\sigma^2\bI)$.
  • $\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\sigma^2\bI)$ ⇒ the $Y_i\sim N(\mu_i,\sigma^2)$ are independent (density factors).
Lecture 3 example Ȳ ⊥ Q via an orthogonal (Helmert) matrixcore

$\bY$ with mean $\bmu$, variance $\Sig$: $\ \bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ ⇔ $\ba'\bY$ is univariate normal for every $\ba\in\mathbb R^n$.

Theorem 2.7 χ² ⇔ idempotentcore

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$, $\bA$ symmetric. Then $Q=\bY'\bA\bY\sim\chi^2_r$ iff $\bA$ is idempotent of rank $r$.

Proof (lecture)

"If": $\bA=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda\mathbf T'$, $\mathbf T$ orthogonal, $\boldsymbol\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(1,\dots,1,0,\dots,0)$ with $r$ ones (A.6.1). $\bZ=\mathbf T'\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$, so $Q=\bZ'\boldsymbol\Lambda\bZ=\sum_{i=1}^rZ_i^2\sim\chi^2_r$.

"Only if": write $\bA=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda\mathbf T'$ with general eigenvalues $\lambda_i$, so $Q=\sum\lambda_iZ_i^2$ and $\E e^{tQ}=\prod_i(1-2t\lambda_i)^{-1/2}$. Matching $(1-2t)^{-r/2}$ forces $r$ of the $\lambda_i$ to be 1 and the rest 0. Then $\boldsymbol\Lambda^2=\boldsymbol\Lambda$, so $\bA^2=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda^2\mathbf T'=\bA$.

Corollary

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$, $\bA$ symmetric, $\bY'\bA\bY\sim\chi^2_r$ ⇒ $\bY'(\bI_n-\bA)\bY\sim\chi^2_{n-r}$ (A.6.3).

Scaling to σ²

Theorem 2.7 is stated for $N(\bzero,\bI)$. For $\bY\sim N(\mu\bone,\sigma^2\bI)$ or $N(\bX\bbeta,\sigma^2\bI)$, first standardize: $\bZ=(\bY-\bmu)/\sigma$, and check that $\bA\bmu=\bzero$ so the quadratic form in $\bY$ equals $\sigma^2\bZ'\bA\bZ$. The lecture examples always do this step.

Lecture 5 example (n−1)S²/σ² ∼ χ²ₙ₋₁core

$\bZ=(\bY-\mu\bone)/\sigma\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI)$ and $(\bY-\bar\bY)/\sigma=(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)\bZ$. So $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2=\bZ'(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)'(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)\bZ=\bZ'(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)\bZ$. The matrix is symmetric idempotent with trace $n-1$; Theorem 2.7.

Lecture 5 example independence of two χ² formscore

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$, $\bA,\bB$ symmetric, $\bY'\bA\bY$ and $\bY'\bB\bY$ both χ². Then they are independent iff $\bA\bB=\bzero$.

Proof (lecture)

"If": $\bA\bB=\bzero$ ⇒ $\bA\bY\perp\bB\bY$ (Theorem 2.5). Theorem 2.7 makes $\bA,\bB$ idempotent, so $\bY'\bA\bY=\bY'\bA'\bA\bY=\|\bA\bY\|^2$ and $\bY'\bB\bY=\|\bB\bY\|^2$, functions of independent vectors.

"Only if": the sum of independent χ²'s is χ², so $\bY'(\bA+\bB)\bY$ is χ² and $\bA+\bB$ is idempotent. Expanding gives $\bA\bB+\bB\bA=\bzero$. Multiply by $\bA$ on the left: $\bA\bB+\bA\bB\bA=\bzero$; on the right: $\bA\bB\bA+\bB\bA=\bzero$. Hence $\bA\bB=\bB\bA$, and then $\bA\bB=\bB\bA=\bzero$.

Lecture 5 example difference of χ² formscore

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI)$, $Q_i=\bY'\bP_i\bY\sim\chi^2_{r_i}$ with $\bP_i$ symmetric, and $Q_1-Q_2\ge0$. Then $Q_1-Q_2\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$, independent of $Q_2$.

Proof: Theorem 2.7 makes $\bP_1,\bP_2$ projections; $Q_1-Q_2=\bY'(\bP_1-\bP_2)\bY\ge0$ means $\bP_1-\bP_2\succeq0$. By A.6.5 it is a projection of rank $r_1-r_2$, so Theorem 2.7 gives the χ². Also $\bP_1\bP_2=\bP_2$, so $(\bP_1-\bP_2)\bP_2=\bzero$, and the previous example gives independence. (This is the structure behind $\mathrm{RSS}_H-\mathrm{RSS}$.)

Theorem 2.8 general Σcore

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\Sig)$, $\bA$ symmetric. Then $\bY'\bA\bY\sim\chi^2_r$ iff $r$ eigenvalues of $\bA\Sig$ are 1 and the other $n-r$ are 0.

Proof (lecture)

$\Sig=\bR\bR'$ with $\bR$ $n\times m$, so $\bY=\bR\bZ$, $\bZ\sim N_m(\bzero,\bI_m)$ (Definition 2.2) and $Q=\bZ'(\bR'\bA\bR)\bZ$. By Theorem 2.7, $Q\sim\chi^2_r$ iff $\bR'\bA\bR$ is idempotent of rank $r$. $\bR'\bA\bR$ and $\bA\bR\bR'=\bA\Sig$ share nonzero eigenvalues, and $\tr(\bA\Sig)=\tr(\bR'\bA\bR)=r$, so $\bA\Sig$ has $r$ unit eigenvalues and the rest zero.

Corollary Σ positive definite

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\Sig)$, $\Sig\succ0$, $\bA$ symmetric: $\bY'\bA\bY\sim\chi^2_r$ iff $\bA\Sig$ is idempotent of rank $r$.

Proof: $\bR$ is invertible; conjugate $(\bR'\bA\bR)^2=\bR'\bA\bR$ by $(\bR')^{-1}(\cdot)\bR'$ to get $(\bA\Sig)^2=\bA\Sig$.

L6Least squares estimation

Normal equations core

Minimizing $\beps'\beps$ makes $\bY-\bX\bhat\perp\Omega=\mathcal C(\bX)$, i.e. $\bX'(\bY-\bX\bhat)=\bzero$:

$$\bX'\bX\bhat=\bX'\bY,\qquad \bhat=(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'\bY.$$

Calculus route: $\tfrac{d}{d\bbeta}(\bbeta'\ba)=\ba$, $\tfrac{d}{d\bbeta}(\bbeta'\bA\bbeta)=(\bA+\bA')\bbeta$, so $\tfrac{d}{d\bbeta}\beps'\beps=-2\bX'\bY+2\bX'\bX\bbeta$.

Why β̂ minimizes (lecture)

Write $\bY-\bX\bbeta=(\bY-\bX\bhat)+(\bX\bhat-\bX\bbeta)$. With $\bP=\bX(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'$ the cross term is $\bY'(\bI-\bP)\bP(\bY-\bX\bbeta)=0$ since $\bP^2=\bP$. So $\beps'\beps=\|\bY-\bX\bhat\|^2+\|\bX\bhat-\bX\bbeta\|^2\ge\|\bY-\bX\bhat\|^2$, with equality iff $\bbeta=\bhat$ (full rank).

Theorem 3.1 hat matrixcore

$\rank\bX=p$, $\bP=\bX(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'$:

  1. $\bP$ and $\bI_n-\bP$ are symmetric and idempotent.
  2. $\rank(\bI_n-\bP)=\tr(\bI_n-\bP)=n-p$.
  3. $\bP\bX=\bX$, $\bP$ projects onto $\Omega=\mathcal C(\bX)$; $(\bI_n-\bP)\bX=\bzero$, so $\bI_n-\bP$ projects onto $\Omega^\perp$.
Proof

(i) $\bP^2=\bX(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'\bX(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'=\bP$; A.6.3 for $\bI-\bP$. (ii) $\tr\bP=\tr((\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'\bX)=\tr\bI_p=p$; A.6.2. (iii) direct multiplication.

Consequences
  • $\hat\bY=\bP\bY$, $\be=(\bI-\bP)\bY$, $\bX'\be=\bzero$, $\hat\bY'\be=0$.
  • With an intercept column $\bone_n$: $\sum_i(Y_i-\hat Y_i)=\bone'\be=0$ (HW 3a.2).
  • $\mathrm{RSS}=\be'\be=\bY'(\bI-\bP)\bY=\bY'\bY-\bhat'\bX'\bX\bhat$.
  • $\bY=\bone\beta_0+\beps$ ⇒ $\hat\beta_0=(\bone'\bone)^{-1}\bone'\bY=\bar Y$.

Theorem 3.2 Gauss–Markovcore

Assume $\E\beps=\bzero$, $\Var\varepsilon_i=\sigma^2$, $\Cov(\varepsilon_i,\varepsilon_j)=0$ ($i\ne j$). If $\bc'\bbeta$ is estimable, then the (unique) $\bc'\bhat$ has minimum variance among all linear unbiased estimators: it is the BLUE.

Proof (lecture)

Let $\ba'\bY$ be unbiased for $\bc'\bbeta$, so $\bc=\bX'\ba$. Let $\bP$ be the projection onto $\mathcal C(\bX)$ and split $\ba'\bY=\ba'\bP\bY+\ba'(\bI-\bP)\bY$. Since $\Cov(\ba'\bP\bY,\ba'(\bI-\bP)\bY)=\sigma^2\ba'\bP(\bI-\bP)\ba=0$,

$\Var(\ba'\bY)=\Var(\ba'\bP\bY)+\Var(\ba'(\bI-\bP)\bY)\ge\Var(\ba'\bP\bY)=\Var(\ba'\bX\bhat)=\Var(\bc'\bhat)$.

Notes on Gauss–Markov Lecture 8
  • No distributional assumption on the $\varepsilon_i$ is needed. Under normality, $\bc'\bhat$ has minimum variance among all unbiased estimators, not just linear ones.
  • $\E(\bc'\bhat)=\bc'\bbeta$ for estimable $\bc'\bbeta$ does not give $\E\bhat=\bbeta$ when $\rank\bX\lt p$: then $\E\bhat=(\bX'\bX)^-\bX'\bX\bbeta$ depends on the g-inverse.
  • Corollary: if $\rank\bX=p$, $\bc'\bhat$ is the BLUE for every $\bc\in\mathbb R^p$.
Consistency Lecture 8 proposition

$\varepsilon_i$ iid $(0,\sigma^2)$. If the smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_p(n)$ of $\bX'\bX$ tends to $\infty$, then $\bhat\xrightarrow{P}\bbeta$.

Proof (lecture)

Eventually $\lambda_p(n)\gt0$, so $\bhat$ is unique. The largest eigenvalue of $(\bX'\bX)^{-1}$ is $1/\lambda_p(n)$, so for any $\ba$: $\Var(\ba'\bhat)=\sigma^2\ba'(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\ba\le\sigma^2\ba'\ba/\lambda_p(n)\to0$. L8Estimating σ²

Theorem 3.3 S² is unbiasedcore

$\E\beps=\bzero$, $\Var\beps=\sigma^2\bI_n$, $\rank\bX=p$ ⇒ $\E S^2=\sigma^2$, where $S^2=\dfrac{\mathrm{RSS}}{n-p}=\dfrac{(\bY-\bX\bhat)'(\bY-\bX\bhat)}{n-p}$.

Proof (lecture): $\mathrm{RSS}=\bY'(\bI-\bP)\bY$ with $\E\bY=\bX\bbeta$, $\Var\bY=\sigma^2\bI$. Theorem 1.5: $\E\,\mathrm{RSS}=\tr((\bI-\bP)\sigma^2\bI)+(\bX\bbeta)'(\bI-\bP)\bX\bbeta=\sigma^2(n-p)$.

Theorem 3.4 optimality of S²

$Y_1,\dots,Y_n$ independent with common variance $\sigma^2$ and common third and fourth central moments $\mu_3,\mu_4$; $\E\bY=\bX\bbeta$, $\rank\bX=p$. Then $S^2$ is the unique nonnegative quadratic unbiased estimator of $\sigma^2$ with minimum variance when $\mu_4=3\sigma^4$ or when the diagonal elements of $\bP$ are all equal.

Proof idea (lecture): use the formulas for $\E(\bY'\bA\bY)$ and $\Var(\bY'\bA\bY)$ (Theorems 1.5, 1.6); HW 3c.2 carries this out for $\bX=\bone_n$. Under normality $S^2$ is minimum-variance among all unbiased estimators, not only quadratic ones.

Variance of S²

Under normality $\Var S^2=2\sigma^4/(n-p)$ (since $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$). In general, Theorem 1.6 with $\bA=\bI-\bP$, $\bA\bX\bbeta=\bzero$ gives $\Var\,\mathrm{RSS}=2\sigma^4(n-p)+(\mu_4-3\sigma^4)\sum_i(1-p_{ii})^2$.

L9Distribution theory and maximum likelihood

Theorem 3.5 core

If $\bY\sim N_n(\bX\bbeta,\sigma^2\bI_n)$ and $\rank\bX=p$:

  1. $\bhat\sim N_p(\bbeta,\sigma^2(\bX'\bX)^{-1})$;
  2. $(\bhat-\bbeta)'\bX'\bX(\bhat-\bbeta)/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_p$;
  3. $\bhat$ is independent of $S^2=\be'\be/(n-p)$;
  4. $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2=(n-p)S^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$.
Proof (lecture)

(i) $\bhat=\bC\bY$; Theorem 2.2. (ii) $\Var(\bhat)^{-1}=\bX'\bX/\sigma^2$; Theorem 2.9. (iii) $\bhat$ and $\be=(\bI-\bP)\bY$ are jointly normal with $\Cov(\bhat,\be)=(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'\sigma^2\bI(\bI-\bP)=\bzero$ because $\bX'=\bX'\bP$; Theorem 2.5.

(iv) Method 1: $(\bI-\bP)\bX\bbeta=\bzero$, so $\mathrm{RSS}=\beps'(\bI-\bP)\beps$ with $\bI-\bP$ a projection of rank $n-p$; Theorem 2.7. Method 2: $Q_1=\beps'\beps=\underbrace{\|\bY-\bX\bhat\|^2}_{Q}+\underbrace{\|\bX\bhat-\bX\bbeta\|^2}_{Q_2}$ with $Q_1/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_n$, $Q_2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_p$ by (ii), $Q\perp Q_2$ by (iii); the χ² subtraction lemma (Lecture 2) gives $Q/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$.

Remarks t statistics

$\hat\beta_j\sim N(\beta_j,\sigma^2[(\bX'\bX)^{-1}]_{j+1,j+1})$ and

$$\frac{\hat\beta_j-\beta_j}{S\sqrt{[(\bX'\bX)^{-1}]_{j+1,j+1}}}=\frac{(\hat\beta_j-\beta_j)/\big(\sigma\sqrt{[\cdot]_{j+1,j+1}}\big)}{\sqrt{\frac{(n-p)S^2/\sigma^2}{n-p}}}\sim t_{n-p}.$$

Construction: $Z\sim N(0,1)\perp U\sim\chi^2_n$ ⇒ $Z/\sqrt{U/n}\sim t_n$. Independence of numerator and denominator is Theorem 3.5(iii).

Maximum likelihood

$\log L=-\tfrac n2\log2\pi-\tfrac n2\log\sigma^2-\tfrac1{2\sigma^2}(\mathbf y-\bX\bbeta)'(\mathbf y-\bX\bbeta)$. Setting both derivatives to zero:

$$\bhat_{\rm MLE}=(\bX'\bX)^{-1}\bX'\bY=\bhat_{\rm LSE},\qquad \hat\sigma^2_{\rm MLE}=\frac{\mathrm{RSS}}{n}=\frac{(n-p)S^2}{n}.$$

$\E\hat\sigma^2_{\rm MLE}=\frac{n-p}{n}\sigma^2$ (biased, asymptotically unbiased). $\Var S^2=\frac{2\sigma^4}{n-p}$, $\Var\hat\sigma^2_{\rm MLE}=\frac{2(n-p)\sigma^4}{n^2}$.

Orthogonal design core

$\bX=[\bx^0,\dots,\bx^{p-1}]$ with $(\bx^i)'\bx^j=0$ for $i\ne j$. Then $\bX'\bX=\mathrm{diag}((\bx^j)'\bx^j)$ and

$$\hat\beta_j=\frac{(\bx^j)'\bY}{(\bx^j)'\bx^j},\qquad \mathrm{RSS}=\bY'\bY-\sum_j\hat\beta_j^2\,(\bx^j)'\bx^j.$$

Lecture 10 example orthogonal designs are optimal

L12Generalized least squares

GLS estimator

$\Var\beps=\sigma^2\bV$, $\bV$ PD, $\bV=\bK\bK'$ with $\bK$ nonsingular. Set $\bZ=\bK^{-1}\bY$, $\bB=\bK^{-1}\bX$, $\boldsymbol\eta=\bK^{-1}\beps$, so $\bZ=\bB\bbeta+\boldsymbol\eta$ with $\Var\boldsymbol\eta=\sigma^2\bI$. OLS on the transformed model:

$$\bbeta^*=(\bB'\bB)^{-1}\bB'\bZ=(\bX'\bV^{-1}\bX)^{-1}\bX'\bV^{-1}\bY,\quad \E\bbeta^*=\bbeta,\quad \Var\bbeta^*=\sigma^2(\bX'\bV^{-1}\bX)^{-1}.$$

$\bbeta^*$ minimizes $(\bY-\bX\bbeta)'\bV^{-1}(\bY-\bX\bbeta)$; $\ \mathrm{RSS}^*=(\bY-\bX\bbeta^*)'\bV^{-1}(\bY-\bX\bbeta^*)$.

Properties
  • If $\bc'\bbeta$ is estimable, $\bc'\bbeta^*$ is its unique BLUE (Gauss–Markov on the transformed model).
  • OLS $\bc'\bhat$ is still unbiased but $\Var(\bc'\bhat)\ge\Var(\bc'\bbeta^*)$.
  • Normal errors: $\bbeta^*_{\rm MLE}=\bbeta^*$, $\hat\sigma^{2*}_{\rm MLE}=\mathrm{RSS}^*/n$.
  • Weighted LS: $\bV=\mathrm{diag}(v_i)$ minimizes $\sum\varepsilon_i^2/v_i$. For a straight line with $w_i=1/\sigma_i^2$: $\beta_1^*=\dfrac{\sum w_i(x_i-\bar x_w)(Y_i-\bar Y_w)}{\sum w_i(x_i-\bar x_w)^2}$, $\beta_0^*=\bar Y_w-\beta_1^*\bar x_w$, with weighted means $\bar x_w,\bar Y_w$.
