The exam is closed book, you won't need theorems word for word, and there's no heavy matrix algebra. What it tests is recognizing the trick that makes a short proof go through. So study in this order:

  1. Learn the 18 tricks below. For each one, know the signal in a problem statement that calls for it.
  2. Write the core proofs. The exam is proofs of results with almost no calculation. Work through Proofs to Know Cold (Part II): cover each proof, write it from the statement, check, and tick it off in the checklist.
  3. Drill recognition. Open a homework drill or a new drill, read only the statement, and name the trick(s) before writing anything. The solution opens with the trick numbers, so you can check yourself.
  4. Carry in a handful of facts (listed below). These are distributional facts the tricks need, not theorems to recite.
  5. Use chapters 1–3 further down only as reference when a trick feels unfamiliar.

2The Eighteen Tricks

Grouped by what they accomplish. Each lists the signal that calls for it, the move, and where it already appeared.

2.1Rewriting the Object

T1 Add and subtract the mean

Spot it: an expectation of a square or outer product centered somewhere other than the mean, such as $\E\|\bX-\ba\|^2$ or $\|\bY-\bX\bbeta\|^2$.

Move: $\bX-\ba=(\bX-\bmu)+(\bmu-\ba)$. Expand; the cross term has a factor $\E(\bX-\bmu)=\bzero$ (or is orthogonal, see T2).

Seen in: HW 1a.1, Thm 1.5 proof 1, why $\bhat$ minimizes RSS.

T2 Pythagoras: kill the cross term

Spot it: "show X minimizes", "show RSS splits", or comparing two fits.

Move: write $\bY-\bX\bbeta=(\bY-\bX\bhat)+\bX(\bhat-\bbeta)$. The cross term vanishes because residuals are orthogonal to the column space: $\bX^\top\be=\bzero$ (normal equations). For restricted LS the cross term vanishes because $\bA(\bhat_H-\bbeta)=\bzero$. Conclude $\|\cdot\|^2=\text{fixed}+\text{nonnegative}$.

Seen in: L6 minimization, L11 identities, Thm 3.5(iv) method 2, Gauss–Markov.

T3 Shift invariance: set μ = 0

Spot it: the statistic only involves differences $X_i-X_j$ or deviations $X_i-\bar X$.

Move: replace $X_i$ by $X_i-\mu$; the statistic is unchanged, so assume $\mu=0$ and every $\bmu^\top\bA\bmu$ or mean term disappears. In matrix form this is $\bA\bone=\bzero$.

Seen in: L2 successive differences, L2 equicorrelation, HW 1b.5, 2b.8.

T4 A scalar is its own trace

Spot it: expectation of a quadratic form $\bX^\top\bA\bX$.

Move: $\E\bX^\top\bA\bX=\E\tr(\bA\bX\bX^\top)=\tr(\bA\,\E\bX\bX^\top)=\tr(\bA\Sig)+\bmu^\top\bA\bmu$. With $\Sig=\sigma^2\bI$ this is $\sigma^2\tr\bA$, so you only need the diagonal of $\bA$.

Seen in: Thm 1.5, Thm 3.3 ($\E S^2$), HW 1b.5(b), Misc 1.3.

T5 Write it as a matrix times Y

Spot it: sample means, deviations, differences, estimators: anything linear in the data.

Move: $\bar Y=\tfrac1n\bone^\top\bY$, deviations $=\bC\bY$ with $\bC=\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ$, differences $=\bA\bY$, $\bhat=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bY$, $\be=(\bI-\bP)\bY$. Then $\Var(\bA\bY)=\bA\Sig\bA^\top$ and $\Cov(\bA\bY,\bB\bY)=\bA\Sig\bB^\top$ do the work, and normality passes through.

Seen in: HW 1a.3, 2c.2, every Thm 3.5 part.

T6 Quadratic form as a squared norm

Spot it: you need independence or a distribution for $\bY^\top\bA\bY$ and $\bA$ is a projection ($\bA=\bA^\top=\bA^2$).

Move: $\bY^\top\bA\bY=\bY^\top\bA^\top\bA\bY=\|\bA\bY\|^2$. A quadratic form becomes a function of the linear vector $\bA\bY$, and T9 applies.

Seen in: L5 independence example, $S^2=\|\bC\bY\|^2/(n-1)$, $\mathrm{RSS}=\|(\bI-\bP)\bY\|^2$.

2.2Getting a Distribution

T7 Standardize first

Spot it: normal data with mean $\mu\bone$, $\bX\bbeta$, or covariance $\Sig\ne\bI$.

Move: $\bZ=(\bY-\bmu)/\sigma$ or $\bZ=\Sig^{-1/2}(\bY-\bmu)$, so $\bZ\sim N(\bzero,\bI)$. Check $\bA\bmu=\bzero$ so the form in $\bY$ equals $\sigma^2\bZ^\top\bA\bZ$.

Seen in: L5 $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2$, Thm 2.9, Thm 3.5(iv) ($\mathrm{RSS}=\beps^\top(\bI-\bP)\beps$).

T8 Idempotent ⇒ χ², rank = trace

Spot it: "show $Q\sim\chi^2$" or "find $c$ so that $cQ$ is χ²".

Move: write $Q=\bZ^\top\bA\bZ$ with $\bZ\sim N(\bzero,\bI)$, check $\bA^2=\bA$, read degrees of freedom off $\tr\bA$. Identities that make the check one line: $\bJ^2=n\bJ$, $\bC\bJ=\bzero$, $\bP\bX=\bX$. To rule out χ², find one entry where $\bA^2\ne\bA$.

Seen in: HW 2d.4, Misc 2.8, L5 examples, Thm 3.5(iv).

T9 Zero covariance ⇒ independence (normal only)

Spot it: "show U and V are independent" with normal data.

Move: express both as functions of linear forms $\bA\bY$, $\bB\bY$ (T5, T6). Compute $\bA\Sig\bB^\top$; if zero, the linear forms are independent, and so are any functions of them. Say "jointly normal" explicitly: it is the condition graders look for.

Seen in: L4 $\bar Y\perp Q$, HW 2c.2, Thm 3.5(iii), Misc 2.8.

T10 Factor the joint MGF

Spot it: independence must be shown "from first principles" or without Chapter 2 machinery; or you must identify a distribution.

Move: compute $\E e^{sU+\bt^\top\mathbf V}$, rearrange the exponent (often completing a square, using $\sum(t_i-\bar t)=0$), and split it into (function of $s$)·(function of $\bt$). For distributions, match a known MGF and invoke uniqueness.

Seen in: HW 2b.8, Thm 2.4 proof, Cramér–Wold.

T11 χ² subtraction by MGF

Spot it: $Q_1=Q+Q_2$ with $Q_1,Q_2$ known χ² and $Q\perp Q_2$.

Move: $M_{Q_1}=M_QM_{Q_2}$, so $M_Q=(1-2t)^{-(r_1-r_2)/2}$ and $Q\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$.

Seen in: L2 example, Thm 3.5(iv) method 2.

T12 Rotate with an orthogonal matrix

Spot it: iid $N(0,1)$ (or $N(0,\sigma^2\bI)$) data and a sum of squares minus something.

Move: $\bX=\bP\bZ$ with $\bP$ orthogonal is still $N(\bzero,\bI)$ and $\|\bX\|=\|\bZ\|$. Choose the first row $\bone^\top/\sqrt n$ (Helmert) so $X_1=\sqrt n\bar Z$, giving $\sum(Z_i-\bar Z)^2=\sum_{i\ge2}X_i^2$. Eigen-decomposition $\bA=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda\mathbf T^\top$ is the same idea.

Seen in: L3 $\bar Y\perp Q$, Thm 2.7 proof.

T13 Show normality via every linear combination

Spot it: "show this stacked vector is multivariate normal".

Move: for any $\ba$, $\ba^\top\bW$ is a sum of independent normals, hence normal (Cramér–Wold).

Seen in: HW Misc 2.9.

2.3Optimizing and Comparing

T14 Unbiased for all parameters ⇒ constraints

Spot it: "find the minimum-variance unbiased linear/quadratic estimator".

Move: unbiasedness for every $\bbeta$ (or $\theta,\sigma^2$) turns into equations: $\bX^\top\ba=\bc$, or $\bone^\top\ba=1$, or $\tr\bA=1,\ \bone^\top\bA\bone=0$. Then write $\ba=\ba_0+\mathbf d$ with $\ba_0$ the candidate; the constraint kills the cross term (T2) so $\|\ba\|^2=\|\ba_0\|^2+\|\mathbf d\|^2$.

Seen in: HW 3b.3, 3c.2, Gauss–Markov.

T15 Difference is a square, so ≥ 0

Spot it: "show $\Var\tilde\theta\ge\Var\hat\theta$", "deduce each diagonal is smaller".

Move: write the difference as $\sigma^2\mathbf M^\top\mathbf K\mathbf M$ with $\mathbf K\succ0$, or as $\Var(\text{orthogonal piece})$; a PSD matrix has nonnegative diagonal. For scalars, Cauchy–Schwarz: $(\sum x_i)^2\le n\sum x_i^2$.

Seen in: HW 3g.3, 3c.2, Gauss–Markov, ratio estimator drill.

T16 MSE = variance + bias²

Spot it: comparing estimators of $\sigma^2$, or any "smaller mean-squared error".

Move: if $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_m$, then $c\,\mathrm{RSS}$ has mean $cm\sigma^2$ and variance $2c^2m\sigma^4$. Plug in.

Seen in: HW 3c.1, MLE vs $S^2$.

2.4Using Structure

T17 Specialize the regression model

Spot it: a one-sample problem that "should" follow from a regression theorem, or a small design.

Move: take $\bX=\bone_n$ ($p=1$): $\bhat=\bar Y$, $\mathrm{RSS}=\sum(Y_i-\bar Y)^2$, $\bP=\tfrac1n\bJ$. For two-parameter problems write $\bX^\top\bX$ explicitly. With orthogonal or centered columns, $\bX^\top\bX$ is (block) diagonal, so estimates decouple: $\hat\beta_j=\bx_j^\top\bY/\bx_j^\top\bx_j$.

Seen in: HW 3d.1, 3c.2, 3a.4, 3e.2, 3b.1, 3b.4.

T18 Polarization and products of moments

Spot it: a covariance between two quadratic forms, or between a linear and a quadratic form.

Move: $\Cov(U,V)=\tfrac12[\Var(U+V)-\Var U-\Var V]$. For $\E[X_iX_jX_k]$ or $\E[X_iX_jX_kX_l]$ with independent mean-zero $X$'s, only matching indices survive: $\E X_i^3=\mu_3$, pairs give $\sigma^4$.

Seen in: HW Misc 1.5, drill 1.

2.6Facts to Carry In

Not theorems to recite, but the raw material the tricks run on. Know them cold.

χ²

$\chi^2_r=\sum_{i=1}^rZ_i^2$; mean $r$, variance $2r$, MGF $(1-2t)^{-r/2}$. Independent χ²'s add.

Normal

MGF $\exp(\bt^\top\bmu+\tfrac12\bt^\top\Sig\bt)$. $\bA\bY+\mathbf d$ is normal with mean $\bA\bmu+\mathbf d$, covariance $\bA\Sig\bA^\top$.

t and F

$Z/\sqrt{V/k}\sim t_k$ and $(U/m)/(V/k)\sim F_{m,k}$, numerator and denominator independent.

Quadratic forms

$\E\bX^\top\bA\bX=\tr(\bA\Sig)+\bmu^\top\bA\bmu$. Normal, mean zero: $\Var(\bZ^\top\bA\bZ)=2\sigma^4\tr\bA^2$.

Matrices that recur

$\bC=\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ$, $\bJ^2=n\bJ$, $\bC\bone=\bzero$, $\tr\bC=n-1$. $\bP=\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top$, $\bP\bX=\bX$, $\tr\bP=p$.

Regression basics

$\bhat=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bY$, $\Var\bhat=\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}$, $\bX^\top\be=\bzero$, $S^2=\mathrm{RSS}/(n-p)$, $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$, $\bhat\perp S^2$.

4Random Vectors

4.1Covariance of linear transformations

For random vectors $\bX,\bY$ and constant matrices $\bA,\bB$,

$$\Cov(\bA\bX,\bB\bY)=\bA\,\Cov(\bX,\bY)\,\bB^\top,\qquad \Var(\bA\bX)=\bA\,\Var(\bX)\,\bA^\top.$$

4.1.1Proof

Tricks: T5

Denote $\ba:=\E[\bX]$ and $\bb:=\E[\bY]$. By linearity of expectation, $\E[\bA\bX]=\bA\ba$ and $\E[\bB\bY]=\bB\bb$, so

$$\bA\bX-\E[\bA\bX]=\bA(\bX-\ba),\qquad \bB\bY-\E[\bB\bY]=\bB(\bY-\bb).$$

Then, pulling the constant matrices out of the expectation,

$$\Cov(\bA\bX,\bB\bY)=\E\big[\bA(\bX-\ba)(\bY-\bb)^\top\bB^\top\big]=\bA\,\E\big[(\bX-\ba)(\bY-\bb)^\top\big]\,\bB^\top.$$
$$\therefore\ \Cov(\bA\bX,\bB\bY)=\bA\,\Cov(\bX,\bY)\,\bB^\top,\quad\text{and with }\bY=\bX,\ \bB=\bA:\ \Var(\bA\bX)=\bA\,\Var(\bX)\,\bA^\top$$

4.2Mean of a quadratic form

If $\E[\bX]=\bmu$, $\Var(\bX)=\Sig$ and $\bA$ is symmetric, then

$$\E[\bX^\top\bA\bX]=\mathrm{tr}(\bA\Sig)+\bmu^\top\bA\bmu.$$

4.2.1Proof

Tricks: T4

A scalar equals its own trace, and $\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{MN})=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{NM})$, so $\bX^\top\bA\bX=\mathrm{tr}(\bA\bX\bX^\top)$. Trace and expectation are both linear, so they commute:

$$\E[\bX^\top\bA\bX]=\mathrm{tr}\big(\bA\,\E[\bX\bX^\top]\big).$$

Now, from $\Var(\bX)=\E[\bX\bX^\top]-\bmu\bmu^\top$, we have $\E[\bX\bX^\top]=\Sig+\bmu\bmu^\top$. Thus,

$$\E[\bX^\top\bA\bX]=\mathrm{tr}(\bA\Sig)+\mathrm{tr}(\bA\bmu\bmu^\top)=\mathrm{tr}(\bA\Sig)+\bmu^\top\bA\bmu.$$
$$\therefore\ \E[\bX^\top\bA\bX]=\mathrm{tr}(\bA\Sig)+\bmu^\top\bA\bmu$$

4.3Variance matrices are positive semi-definite

$\Var(\bX)=\Sig$ is positive semi-definite. It is positive definite unless $\bc^\top\bX$ is a.s. constant for some $\bc\ne\bzero$.

4.3.1Proof

Tricks: T5

For any constant $\bc$, by the covariance of linear transformations,

$$0\le\Var(\bc^\top\bX)=\bc^\top\Sig\bc.$$

So $\Sig\succeq0$. If $\Sig$ is not positive definite, there is $\bc\ne\bzero$ with $\bc^\top\Sig\bc=0$, i.e. $\Var(\bc^\top\bX)=0$, so $\bc^\top\bX=\E[\bc^\top\bX]$ with probability one.

$\therefore$ $\Sig\succeq0$, and $\Sig\succ0$ whenever no nontrivial linear combination of $\bX$ is constant.

4.4Independent implies uncorrelated, not conversely

If $X$ and $Y$ are independent then $\Cov(X,Y)=0$. The converse fails in general.

4.4.1Proof

Tricks: T5

By independence the joint density factors, so the double integral splits:

$$\E[XY]=\iint xy\,f_X(x)f_Y(y)\,dx\,dy=\int xf_X(x)\,dx\cdot\int yf_Y(y)\,dy=\E[X]\,\E[Y].$$

Thus $\Cov(X,Y)=\E[XY]-\E[X]\E[Y]=0$. For the converse, take $X\sim U(-1,1)$ and $Y=X^2$. Then

$$\Cov(X,Y)=\E[X^3]-\E[X]\,\E[X^2]=0-0=0,$$

yet $Y$ is a function of $X$: for instance $P(Y\lt\tfrac14\mid|X|>\tfrac12)=0\ne\tfrac12=P(Y\lt\tfrac14)$.

$\therefore$ Independence $\Rightarrow$ zero covariance, but zero covariance $\not\Rightarrow$ independence (it does for jointly normal vectors).

5Multivariate Normal

5.1MGF of the multivariate normal

If $\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$, then $M_{\bY}(\bt)=\exp\!\big(\bt^\top\bmu+\tfrac12\bt^\top\Sig\bt\big)$.

5.1.1Proof

Tricks: T10

We can start with $\bZ\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$. Its entries are i.i.d. $N(0,1)$, so

$$M_{\bZ}(\bt)=\E\Big[\prod_{i=1}^ne^{t_iZ_i}\Big]=\prod_{i=1}^ne^{t_i^2/2}=e^{\bt^\top\bt/2}.$$

Now, write $\bY=\Sig^{1/2}\bZ+\bmu$ and let $\mathbf s:=\Sig^{1/2}\bt$ (using that $\Sig^{1/2}$ is symmetric). Then,

$$\E[e^{\bt^\top\bY}]=e^{\bt^\top\bmu}\,\E[e^{\mathbf s^\top\bZ}]=\exp\big(\bt^\top\bmu+\tfrac12\mathbf s^\top\mathbf s\big).$$
$$\therefore\ M_{\bY}(\bt)=\exp\big(\bt^\top\bmu+\tfrac12\bt^\top\Sig\bt\big)$$

5.2Linear transformations stay normal

If $\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ and $\bC$ is $m\times n$ of rank $m$, then $\bC\bY+\mathbf d\sim N_m(\bC\bmu+\mathbf d,\ \bC\Sig\bC^\top)$.

5.2.1Proof

Tricks: T10

Let $\mathbf s:=\bC^\top\bt$. Then,

$$\E\big[e^{\bt^\top(\bC\bY+\mathbf d)}\big]=e^{\bt^\top\mathbf d}\,M_{\bY}(\mathbf s)=\exp\big(\bt^\top\mathbf d+\mathbf s^\top\bmu+\tfrac12\mathbf s^\top\Sig\mathbf s\big)=\exp\big(\bt^\top(\bC\bmu+\mathbf d)+\tfrac12\bt^\top\bC\Sig\bC^\top\bt\big).$$

This is the MGF of $N_m(\bC\bmu+\mathbf d,\bC\Sig\bC^\top)$, and the MGF determines the distribution.

$$\therefore\ \bC\bY+\mathbf d\sim N_m(\bC\bmu+\mathbf d,\ \bC\Sig\bC^\top)$$

5.3Cramér–Wold device

$\bY$ (mean $\bmu$, variance $\Sig$) is $N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ if and only if $\ba^\top\bY$ is univariate normal for every $\ba\in\mathbb R^n$.

5.3.1Proof

Tricks: T13

($\Leftarrow$) $\ba^\top\bY$ is normal with mean $\ba^\top\bmu$ and variance $\ba^\top\Sig\ba$, so evaluating its MGF at $1$,

$$\E[e^{\ba^\top\bY}]=\exp\big(\ba^\top\bmu+\tfrac12\ba^\top\Sig\ba\big)\quad\text{for every }\ba.$$

This is $M_{\bY}(\ba)$ for $N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$, so by uniqueness $\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$.

($\Rightarrow$) Write $\bY=\bA\bZ+\bmu$ with $\bZ\sim N_m(\bzero,\bI_m)$. Then $\ba^\top\bY=(\bA^\top\ba)^\top\bZ+\ba^\top\bmu$ is a linear combination of independent normals.

$\therefore$ $\bY$ is multivariate normal $\iff$ every linear combination $\ba^\top\bY$ is normal.

5.4Zero covariance iff independence (jointly normal)

If $\bY=(\bY_1^\top,\bY_2^\top)^\top\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$, then $\bY_1\perp\!\!\!\perp\bY_2$ if and only if $\Sig_{12}=\bzero$.

5.4.1Proof

Tricks: T10

Partition $\bt=(\bt_1^\top,\bt_2^\top)^\top$. Expanding the quadratic form,

$$\bt^\top\Sig\bt=\bt_1^\top\Sig_{11}\bt_1+2\bt_1^\top\Sig_{12}\bt_2+\bt_2^\top\Sig_{22}\bt_2.$$

($\Leftarrow$) If $\Sig_{12}=\bzero$, the cross term drops and

$$M_{\bY}(\bt)=\exp\big(\bt_1^\top\bmu_1+\tfrac12\bt_1^\top\Sig_{11}\bt_1\big)\cdot\exp\big(\bt_2^\top\bmu_2+\tfrac12\bt_2^\top\Sig_{22}\bt_2\big)=M_{\bY_1}(\bt_1)\,M_{\bY_2}(\bt_2),$$

so the joint MGF factors and the blocks are independent. ($\Rightarrow$) Independence always gives zero covariance.

$$\therefore\ \bY_1\perp\!\!\!\perp\bY_2\iff\Sig_{12}=\bzero$$

5.5Independence of linear forms

If $\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$, then $\bA\bY\perp\!\!\!\perp\bB\bY$ if and only if $\bA\Sig\bB^\top=\bzero$.

5.5.1Proof

Tricks: T5, T9

Stack the two forms:

$$\bZ=\begin{bmatrix}\bA\bY\\\bB\bY\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\bA\\\bB\end{bmatrix}\bY.$$

$\bZ$ is a linear transformation of a normal vector, so it is jointly normal, and its off-diagonal covariance block is $\Cov(\bA\bY,\bB\bY)=\bA\Sig\bB^\top$. By the previous result, the blocks are independent exactly when this is zero.

$$\therefore\ \bA\bY\perp\!\!\!\perp\bB\bY\iff\bA\Sig\bB^\top=\bzero$$

5.6Sample mean and sample variance (rotation proof)

If $\bY\sim N_n(\mu\bone_n,\sigma^2\bI_n)$ and $Q=\frac1{\sigma^2}\sum_i(Y_i-\bar Y)^2$, then $\bar Y\perp\!\!\!\perp Q$ and $Q\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$.

5.6.1Proof

Tricks: T7, T12

Standardize: $Z_i:=(Y_i-\mu)/\sigma$, so $\bZ\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$, $\bar Z=(\bar Y-\mu)/\sigma$, and

$$Q=\sum_i(Z_i-\bar Z)^2=\sum_iZ_i^2-n\bar Z^2.$$

Now, take an orthogonal $\bP$ whose first row is $\bone_n^\top/\sqrt n$, and let $\bX:=\bP\bZ\sim N_n(\bzero,\bP\bP^\top)=N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$. Then $X_1=\sqrt n\,\bar Z$ and $\sum_iX_i^2=\bZ^\top\bP^\top\bP\bZ=\sum_iZ_i^2$. Consequently,

$$Q=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i^2-X_1^2=\sum_{i=2}^nX_i^2.$$

$\therefore$ $Q\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$, and $\bar Y$ depends only on $X_1$, which is independent of $X_2,\dots,X_n$, so $\bar Y\perp\!\!\!\perp Q$.

Other routes worth knowing: factor the joint MGF (HW 2.B.8), zero covariance of $\bar Y$ and $\bC\bY$ (HW 2.C.2), or Theorem 3.5 with $\bX=\bone_n$ (HW 3.D.1).

6Quadratic Forms and Chi-Square

6.1Idempotent iff chi-square

If $\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$ and $\bA$ is symmetric, then $\bY^\top\bA\bY\sim\chi^2_r$ if and only if $\bA$ is idempotent of rank $r$.

6.1.1Proof

Tricks: T8, T12

Write $\bA=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda\mathbf T^\top$ with $\mathbf T$ orthogonal and $\boldsymbol\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)$, and let $\bZ:=\mathbf T^\top\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$. Then,

$$\bY^\top\bA\bY=\bZ^\top\boldsymbol\Lambda\bZ=\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_iZ_i^2.$$

($\Leftarrow$) Idempotent of rank $r$ means $r$ eigenvalues equal $1$ and the rest $0$, so this is a sum of $r$ independent $Z_i^2$: $\chi^2_r$.

($\Rightarrow$) Matching MGFs,

$$\prod_{i=1}^n(1-2t\lambda_i)^{-1/2}=(1-2t)^{-r/2}$$

near $t=0$ forces $r$ of the $\lambda_i$ to be $1$ and the rest $0$. Then $\boldsymbol\Lambda^2=\boldsymbol\Lambda$, so $\bA^2=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda^2\mathbf T^\top=\bA$.

$$\therefore\ \bY^\top\bA\bY\sim\chi^2_r\iff\bA^2=\bA,\ \mathit{rank}(\bA)=r$$

6.2Chi-square subtraction lemma

If $Q_1\sim\chi^2_{r_1}$, $Q_2\sim\chi^2_{r_2}$ with $r_1>r_2$, and $Q=Q_1-Q_2$ is independent of $Q_2$, then $Q\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$.

6.2.1Proof

Tricks: T11

Since $Q_1=Q+Q_2$ with $Q\perp\!\!\!\perp Q_2$, the MGF factors:

$$(1-2t)^{-r_1/2}=M_{Q_1}(t)=\E[e^{tQ}]\,\E[e^{tQ_2}]=\E[e^{tQ}]\,(1-2t)^{-r_2/2}.$$

Solving for the MGF of $Q$,

$$\E[e^{tQ}]=(1-2t)^{-(r_1-r_2)/2},\qquad t\lt\tfrac12.$$
$$\therefore\ Q\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$$

6.3Two chi-square forms are independent iff AB = 0

Let $\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$ and $\bA,\bB$ symmetric with $\bY^\top\bA\bY$, $\bY^\top\bB\bY$ both chi-square. They are independent if and only if $\bA\bB=\bzero$.

6.3.1Proof

Tricks: T6, T9

($\Leftarrow$) Both forms are χ², so $\bA,\bB$ are idempotent, and each form is a squared length:

$$\bY^\top\bA\bY=\bY^\top\bA^\top\bA\bY=\|\bA\bY\|^2,\qquad \bY^\top\bB\bY=\|\bB\bY\|^2.$$

$\Cov(\bA\bY,\bB\bY)=\bA\bB^\top=\bA\bB=\bzero$, so $\bA\bY\perp\!\!\!\perp\bB\bY$, and functions of them are independent.

($\Rightarrow$) A sum of independent χ²'s is χ², so $\bA+\bB$ is idempotent. Expanding $(\bA+\bB)^2=\bA+\bB$ gives $\bA\bB+\bB\bA=\bzero$. Multiplying on the left and on the right by $\bA$:

$$\bA\bB+\bA\bB\bA=\bzero,\qquad \bA\bB\bA+\bB\bA=\bzero\ \Longrightarrow\ \bA\bB=\bB\bA.$$

$\therefore$ Combined with $\bA\bB+\bB\bA=\bzero$, this gives $\bA\bB=\bB\bA=\bzero$, so independence $\iff\bA\bB=\bzero$.

6.4Difference of chi-square forms

Let $\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$, $Q_i=\bY^\top\bP_i\bY\sim\chi^2_{r_i}$ with $\bP_i$ symmetric, and $Q_1-Q_2\ge0$. Then $Q_1-Q_2\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$, independent of $Q_2$.

6.4.1Proof

Tricks: T8, T9

Both forms are χ², so $\bP_1,\bP_2$ are projections. Since $\bY^\top(\bP_1-\bP_2)\bY\ge0$ for every $\bY$, $\bP_1-\bP_2\succeq0$, and by (A.6.5),

$$\bP_1\bP_2=\bP_2\bP_1=\bP_2,\qquad \bP_1-\bP_2\ \text{is a projection}.$$

Its rank is its trace, $r_1-r_2$, so $Q_1-Q_2=\bY^\top(\bP_1-\bP_2)\bY\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$. Further, $(\bP_1-\bP_2)\bP_2=\bP_2-\bP_2=\bzero$, so the previous result gives independence.

$\therefore$ $Q_1-Q_2\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$, independent of $Q_2$.

6.5Mahalanobis form

If $\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ with $\Sig\succ0$, then $(\bY-\bmu)^\top\Sig^{-1}(\bY-\bmu)\sim\chi^2_n$.

6.5.1Proof

Tricks: T7

Write $\bY=\Sig^{1/2}\bZ+\bmu$ with $\bZ\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$. Then,

$$(\bY-\bmu)^\top\Sig^{-1}(\bY-\bmu)=\bZ^\top\Sig^{1/2}\Sig^{-1}\Sig^{1/2}\bZ=\bZ^\top\bZ=\sum_{i=1}^nZ_i^2.$$
$$\therefore\ (\bY-\bmu)^\top\Sig^{-1}(\bY-\bmu)\sim\chi^2_n$$

7Least Squares

7.1The least squares estimate minimizes RSS

If $\mathit{rank}(\bX)=p$ and $\bhat=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bY$, then $\|\bY-\bX\bbeta\|^2\ge\|\bY-\bX\bhat\|^2$ for every $\bbeta$, with equality iff $\bbeta=\bhat$.

7.1.1Proof

Tricks: T1, T2

We can start by adding and subtracting $\bX\bhat$:

$$\bY-\bX\bbeta=(\bY-\bX\bhat)+\bX(\bhat-\bbeta).$$

The cross term vanishes by the normal equations $\bX^\top(\bY-\bX\bhat)=\bzero$:

$$(\bY-\bX\bhat)^\top\bX(\bhat-\bbeta)=\big(\bX^\top(\bY-\bX\bhat)\big)^\top(\bhat-\bbeta)=0.$$

Thus,

$$\|\bY-\bX\bbeta\|^2=\|\bY-\bX\bhat\|^2+\|\bX(\bhat-\bbeta)\|^2.$$

$\therefore$ $\|\bY-\bX\bbeta\|^2\ge\|\bY-\bX\bhat\|^2$, with equality iff $\bX(\bhat-\bbeta)=\bzero$, i.e. $\bbeta=\bhat$ since $\bX$ has full column rank.

7.2Properties of the hat matrix

With $\bP=\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top$ and $\mathit{rank}(\bX)=p$: $\bP$ and $\bI_n-\bP$ are symmetric and idempotent, $\bP\bX=\bX$, and $\mathrm{tr}(\bI_n-\bP)=n-p$.

7.2.1Proof

Tricks: T8

Symmetry follows since $(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}$ is symmetric. For idempotency,

$$\bP^2=\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\underbrace{\bX^\top\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}}_{\bI_p}\bX^\top=\bP,\qquad (\bI_n-\bP)^2=\bI_n-2\bP+\bP^2=\bI_n-\bP.$$

Also $\bP\bX=\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bX=\bX$. For the trace, use $\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{MN})=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{NM})$:

$$\mathrm{tr}(\bP)=\mathrm{tr}\big((\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bX\big)=\mathrm{tr}(\bI_p)=p.$$

$\therefore$ $\bP,\bI_n-\bP$ are projections, $\bP\bX=\bX$, $(\bI_n-\bP)\bX=\bzero$, and $\mathit{rank}(\bI_n-\bP)=\mathrm{tr}(\bI_n-\bP)=n-p$.

7.3Residuals are orthogonal to the columns

The residuals $\be=\bY-\bX\bhat$ satisfy $\bX^\top\be=\bzero$; if $\bX$ has a column of ones, $\sum_ie_i=0$.

7.3.1Proof

Tricks: T2

Using $\bhat=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bY$,

$$\bX^\top\be=\bX^\top\bY-\bX^\top\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bY=\bX^\top\bY-\bX^\top\bY=\bzero_p.$$

If the first column is $\bone_n$, the first entry of $\bX^\top\be$ is $\bone_n^\top\be$.

$$\therefore\ \bX^\top\be=\bzero,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^ne_i=\bone_n^\top\be=0$$

7.4Mean and variance of the least squares estimate

If $\E[\beps]=\bzero$ and $\Var(\beps)=\sigma^2\bI_n$, then $\E[\bhat]=\bbeta$, $\Var(\bhat)=\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}$, and $\Cov(\bhat,\be)=\bzero$.

7.4.1Proof

Tricks: T5

Write $\bhat=\bC\bY$ with $\bC:=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top$. Then $\bC\bX=\bI_p$, so

$$\E[\bhat]=\bC\bX\bbeta=\bbeta,\qquad \Var(\bhat)=\bC(\sigma^2\bI_n)\bC^\top=\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}=\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}.$$

For the covariance with $\be=(\bI_n-\bP)\bY$, note $\bX^\top(\bI_n-\bP)=\bzero$ since $\bP\bX=\bX$:

$$\Cov(\bhat,\be)=\bC(\sigma^2\bI_n)(\bI_n-\bP)^\top=\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top(\bI_n-\bP)=\bzero.$$
$$\therefore\ \E[\bhat]=\bbeta,\qquad \Var(\bhat)=\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1},\qquad \Cov(\bhat,\be)=\bzero$$

7.5Gauss–Markov theorem

Assume $\E[\beps]=\bzero$, $\Var(\beps)=\sigma^2\bI_n$. If $\bc^\top\bbeta$ is estimable, then $\bc^\top\bhat$ has the smallest variance among all linear unbiased estimators of $\bc^\top\bbeta$.

7.5.1Proof

Tricks: T14

Let $\ba^\top\bY$ be unbiased for $\bc^\top\bbeta$. Then $\ba^\top\bX\bbeta=\bc^\top\bbeta$ for every $\bbeta$, so $\bc=\bX^\top\ba$. Now, split along the projection $\bP$:

$$\ba^\top\bY=\ba^\top\bP\bY+\ba^\top(\bI_n-\bP)\bY.$$

The two pieces are uncorrelated, since $\Cov\big(\ba^\top\bP\bY,\ba^\top(\bI_n-\bP)\bY\big)=\sigma^2\ba^\top\bP(\bI_n-\bP)\ba=0$. Thus,

$$\Var(\ba^\top\bY)=\Var(\ba^\top\bP\bY)+\Var(\ba^\top(\bI_n-\bP)\bY)\ \ge\ \Var(\ba^\top\bP\bY).$$

Finally, $\ba^\top\bP\bY=\ba^\top\bX\bhat=\bc^\top\bhat$.

$$\therefore\ \Var(\ba^\top\bY)\ge\Var(\bc^\top\bhat)\quad\text{for every linear unbiased }\ba^\top\bY$$

7.6S² is unbiased for σ²

If $\E[\beps]=\bzero$, $\Var(\beps)=\sigma^2\bI_n$ and $\mathit{rank}(\bX)=p$, then $S^2=\mathrm{RSS}/(n-p)$ satisfies $\E[S^2]=\sigma^2$.

7.6.1Proof

Tricks: T4

We have $\mathrm{RSS}=\be^\top\be=\bY^\top(\bI_n-\bP)\bY$ with $\E[\bY]=\bX\bbeta$ and $\Var(\bY)=\sigma^2\bI_n$. By the mean of a quadratic form,

$$\E[\mathrm{RSS}]=\mathrm{tr}\big((\bI_n-\bP)\sigma^2\bI_n\big)+\bbeta^\top\bX^\top(\bI_n-\bP)\bX\bbeta=\sigma^2(n-p)+0,$$

since $(\bI_n-\bP)\bX=\bzero$.

$$\therefore\ \E[S^2]=\frac{\E[\mathrm{RSS}]}{n-p}=\sigma^2$$

7.7Distribution theory (Theorem 3.5)

If $\bY\sim N_n(\bX\bbeta,\sigma^2\bI_n)$ and $\mathit{rank}(\bX)=p$: (i) $\bhat\sim N_p(\bbeta,\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1})$; (ii) $(\bhat-\bbeta)^\top\bX^\top\bX(\bhat-\bbeta)/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_p$; (iii) $\bhat\perp\!\!\!\perp S^2$; (iv) $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$.

7.7.1Proof

Tricks: T5, T9, T8

(i) $\bhat=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bY$ is a linear transformation of a normal vector, with mean and variance as computed above.

(ii) Since $\Var(\bhat)^{-1}=\bX^\top\bX/\sigma^2$, the form is $(\bhat-\bbeta)^\top\Var(\bhat)^{-1}(\bhat-\bbeta)$, a Mahalanobis form in $p$ dimensions.

(iii) $\bhat$ and $\be=(\bI_n-\bP)\bY$ are jointly normal with $\Cov(\bhat,\be)=\bzero$, so they are independent, and $S^2=\be^\top\be/(n-p)$ is a function of $\be$.

(iv) Since $(\bI_n-\bP)\bX\bbeta=\bzero$, $\mathrm{RSS}=\beps^\top(\bI_n-\bP)\beps$, and $\beps/\sigma\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$ with $\bI_n-\bP$ a projection of rank $n-p$.

$\therefore$ All four parts follow from: linear maps of normals are normal, Mahalanobis forms are χ², zero covariance ⇒ independence, and projections give χ².

7.8The t statistic

Under normality, $\dfrac{\hat\beta_j-\beta_j}{S\sqrt{v_{jj}}}\sim t_{n-p}$, where $v_{jj}$ is the corresponding diagonal entry of $(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}$.

7.8.1Proof

Tricks: T8, T9

By Theorem 3.5(i), $\hat\beta_j\sim N(\beta_j,\sigma^2v_{jj})$, so

$$Z:=\frac{\hat\beta_j-\beta_j}{\sigma\sqrt{v_{jj}}}\sim N(0,1).$$

By (iv), $V:=(n-p)S^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$, and by (iii), $Z\perp\!\!\!\perp V$. Now, dividing numerator and denominator by $\sigma$,

$$\frac{\hat\beta_j-\beta_j}{S\sqrt{v_{jj}}}=\frac{Z}{S/\sigma}=\frac{Z}{\sqrt{V/(n-p)}}.$$

$\therefore$ It is $N(0,1)$ over the root of an independent $\chi^2_{n-p}/(n-p)$, i.e. $t_{n-p}$.

7.9Consistency of the least squares estimate

If $\varepsilon_i\overset{iid}\sim(0,\sigma^2)$ and the smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_p(n)$ of $\bX^\top\bX$ tends to $\infty$, then $\bhat\xrightarrow{P}\bbeta$.

7.9.1Proof

Tricks: T15

For large $n$, $\lambda_p(n)>0$, so $\bhat$ is defined. The largest eigenvalue of $(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}$ is $1/\lambda_p(n)$. Then for any fixed $\ba$, $\E[\ba^\top\bhat]=\ba^\top\bbeta$ and

$$\Var(\ba^\top\bhat)=\sigma^2\ba^\top(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\ba\le\frac{\sigma^2\ba^\top\ba}{\lambda_p(n)}\longrightarrow0.$$

By Chebyshev's inequality, for every $\varepsilon>0$,

$$P\big(|\ba^\top\bhat-\ba^\top\bbeta|\ge\varepsilon\big)\le\frac{\Var(\ba^\top\bhat)}{\varepsilon^2}\longrightarrow0.$$

$\therefore$ Taking $\ba=\mathbf e_i$ gives $\hat\beta_i\xrightarrow{P}\beta_i$ for each $i$, so $\bhat\xrightarrow{P}\bbeta$.

8Restrictions and Generalized Least Squares

8.1The restricted estimate satisfies the restriction

With $\bG=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}$, $\bK=\bA\bG\bA^\top$ and $\bhat_H=\bhat+\bG\bA^\top\bK^{-1}(\bc-\bA\bhat)$: $\bA\bhat_H=\bc$, and $\E[\bhat_H]=\bbeta$ when $\bA\bbeta=\bc$.

8.1.1Proof

Tricks: T5

Multiplying by $\bA$, the bracket cancels:

$$\bA\bhat_H=\bA\bhat+\bK\bK^{-1}(\bc-\bA\bhat)=\bc.$$

$\bhat_H$ is linear in $\bhat$, so taking expectations with $\E[\bhat]=\bbeta$,

$$\E[\bhat_H]=\bbeta+\bG\bA^\top\bK^{-1}(\bc-\bA\bbeta).$$

$\therefore$ $\bA\bhat_H=\bc$, and $\bhat_H$ is unbiased exactly when $\bA\bbeta=\bc$.

8.2Extra residual sum of squares

$\mathrm{RSS}_H=\mathrm{RSS}+\|\hat\bY-\hat\bY_H\|^2$, where $\hat\bY_H=\bX\bhat_H$.

8.2.1Proof

Tricks: T2

Add and subtract $\hat\bY$:

$$\bY-\hat\bY_H=(\bY-\hat\bY)+(\hat\bY-\hat\bY_H).$$

The second piece is $\bX(\bhat-\bhat_H)$, in the column space of $\bX$, and the residuals are orthogonal to it:

$$(\bY-\hat\bY)^\top\bX(\bhat-\bhat_H)=\bY^\top(\bI_n-\bP)\bX(\bhat-\bhat_H)=0.$$
$$\therefore\ \mathrm{RSS}_H=\|\bY-\hat\bY_H\|^2=\|\bY-\hat\bY\|^2+\|\hat\bY-\hat\bY_H\|^2=\mathrm{RSS}+\|\hat\bY-\hat\bY_H\|^2$$

8.3Restriction reduces each variance

$\Var(\bhat_H)=\sigma^2(\bG-\bL)$ with $\bL=\bG\bA^\top\bK^{-1}\bA\bG$, so $\Var(\hat\beta_{Hj})\le\Var(\hat\beta_j)$ for each $j$.

8.3.1Proof

Tricks: T15

The variance formula is HW 3.G.3. For the comparison, since $\bK^{-1}\succ0$, for every $\mathbf v$,

$$\mathbf v^\top\bL\mathbf v=(\bA\bG\mathbf v)^\top\bK^{-1}(\bA\bG\mathbf v)\ge0,$$

so $\bL\succeq0$ and its diagonal entries are nonnegative. Taking $\mathbf v=\mathbf e_j$,

$$\Var(\hat\beta_j)-\Var(\hat\beta_{Hj})=\sigma^2\mathbf e_j^\top\bL\mathbf e_j=\sigma^2\ell_{jj}\ge0.$$
$$\therefore\ \Var(\hat\beta_{Hj})\le\Var(\hat\beta_j)$$

8.4Generalized least squares is BLUE

If $\Var(\beps)=\sigma^2\bV$ with $\bV\succ0$, then $\bbeta^*=(\bX^\top\bV^{-1}\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bV^{-1}\bY$ is unbiased, $\Var(\bbeta^*)=\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bV^{-1}\bX)^{-1}$, and $\bc^\top\bbeta^*$ is the BLUE of $\bc^\top\bbeta$.

8.4.1Proof

Tricks: T14

Write $\bV=\bK\bK^\top$ with $\bK$ nonsingular, and transform:

$$\bZ:=\bK^{-1}\bY,\quad \bB:=\bK^{-1}\bX,\quad \boldsymbol\eta:=\bK^{-1}\beps\ \Longrightarrow\ \bZ=\bB\bbeta+\boldsymbol\eta,\quad \Var(\boldsymbol\eta)=\sigma^2\bK^{-1}\bV\bK^{-\top}=\sigma^2\bI_n.$$

This is the standard model, and its least squares estimate is

$$(\bB^\top\bB)^{-1}\bB^\top\bZ=(\bX^\top\bV^{-1}\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bV^{-1}\bY=\bbeta^*,$$

since $\bK^{-\top}\bK^{-1}=\bV^{-1}$. Consequently, $\E[\bbeta^*]=\bbeta$ and $\Var(\bbeta^*)=\sigma^2(\bB^\top\bB)^{-1}=\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bV^{-1}\bX)^{-1}$. Linear unbiased estimators in $\bY$ are exactly linear unbiased estimators in $\bZ=\bK^{-1}\bY$, so Gauss–Markov applies.

$\therefore$ $\bc^\top\bbeta^*$ is the BLUE of $\bc^\top\bbeta$; in particular $\Var(\bc^\top\bhat)\ge\Var(\bc^\top\bbeta^*)$.

11Linear Regression Analysis, Problem 1.B.2

If $X_1,\dots,X_n$ are independent random variables with common mean $\mu$ and variances $\sigma_1^2,\dots,\sigma_n^2$, prove that $\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2/[n(n-1)]$ is an unbiased estimate of $\Var(\bar X)$.

11.1Solution

Reading the question

If $X_1,\dots,X_n$ are independent random variables with common meanT1 $\mu$ and variances $\sigma_1^2,\dots,\sigma_n^2$, prove that $\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2$T1$/[n(n-1)]$ is an unbiased estimateT4 of $\Var(\bar X)$.

  • T4 Unbiasedness means compute an expectation of a sum of squares.
  • T1 Write $\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2=\sum X_i^2-n\bar X^2$ and use $\E[Z^2]=\Var(Z)+(\E Z)^2$.
  • T1 With a common mean the $\mu^2$ terms cancel, so only the variances survive.

Trick: T1 (sum of squares = raw second moments minus $n\bar X^2$)

By independence,

$$\Var(\bar X)=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^n\sigma_i^2.$$

From $\Var(Z)=\E[Z^2]-(\E[Z])^2$, the second moments are

$$\E[X_i^2]=\sigma_i^2+\mu^2,\qquad \E[\bar X^2]=\Var(\bar X)+\mu^2.$$

Now, using $\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2=\sum_iX_i^2-n\bar X^2$ and linearity, the $\mu^2$ terms cancel:

$$\E\Big[\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2\Big]=\sum_{i=1}^n\sigma_i^2+n\mu^2-n\Var(\bar X)-n\mu^2=n^2\Var(\bar X)-n\Var(\bar X)=n(n-1)\Var(\bar X).$$
$$\therefore\ \E\left[\frac{\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2}{n(n-1)}\right]=\Var(\bar X)$$

12Linear Regression Analysis, Problem 1.B.5 calculation

($\Leftarrow$) If $\ba^\top\bX=b$ a.s. with $\ba\ne\bzero$, then $\ba^\top\Sig\ba=\Var(b)=0$. Since $\Sig\succeq0$, write $\Sig=\Sig^{1/2}\Sig^{1/2}$: $0=\ba^\top\Sig\ba=\|\Sig^{1/2}\ba\|^2$, so $\Sig^{1/2}\ba=\bzero$ and $\Sig\ba=\bzero$.

$\therefore$ $\Sig$ is singular $\iff$ $\ba^\top\bX$ is a.s. constant for some $\ba\ne\bzero$.

46.1.1Part (a)

Note that $\bP\bX=\bX$, so $(\bI_n-\bP)\bX\bbeta=\bzero$ and $\be=(\bI_n-\bP)\beps$. Then,

$$\E[\be]=(\bI_n-\bP)\E[\beps]=\bzero,\qquad \Var(\be)=(\bI_n-\bP)\sigma^2\bI_n(\bI_n-\bP)^\top=\sigma^2(\bI_n-\bP),$$

$$\therefore\ \sum_{i=1}^n\Var(e_i)=\sigma^2\,\mathrm{tr}(\bI_n-\bP)=\sigma^2\big(n-\mathrm{tr}((\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bX)\big)=(n-p)\sigma^2$$

46.1.2Part (b)

since $\bX^\top\bP=(\bP\bX)^\top=\bX^\top$.

$$\therefore\ \be\perp\!\!\!\perp\bhat$$

46.1.3Part (c)

$$\frac{\be^\top\be}{\sigma^2}=\Big(\frac{\beps}\sigma\Big)^\top(\bI_n-\bP)\Big(\frac{\beps}\sigma\Big)\sim\chi^2_{n-p}.$$

Using $\E[\chi^2_\nu]=\nu$ and $\Var(\chi^2_\nu)=2\nu$ with $S^2=\frac{\sigma^2}{n-p}\cdot\frac{\be^\top\be}{\sigma^2}$,

$$\therefore\ \E[S^2]=\frac{\sigma^2}{n-p}(n-p)=\sigma^2,\qquad \Var(S^2)=\frac{\sigma^4}{(n-p)^2}\cdot2(n-p)=\frac{2\sigma^4}{n-p}$$

47.1.1Part (a)

48Matrix Toolkit

Almost every proof reduces to one of these facts.

Trace

$\tr(\bA\bB)=\tr(\bB\bA)$; trace is linear and commutes with $\E$; a scalar is its own trace, so $\bx^\top\bA\bx=\tr(\bA\bx\bx^\top)$.

Symmetrizing

$\bX^\top\bA\bX=\bX^\top\big(\tfrac{\bA+\bA^\top}{2}\big)\bX$, so any quadratic form can use a symmetric matrix. L1

Square root

$\Sig=\bP\boldsymbol\Lambda\bP^\top$, $\Sig^{1/2}=\bP\boldsymbol\Lambda^{1/2}\bP^\top$ is symmetric with $(\Sig^{1/2})^2=\Sig$. L3

Rank of $\bX^\top\bX$

$\rank(\bX^\top\bX)=\rank\bX$. Full column rank ⇒ $\bX^\top\bX$ is positive definite and invertible.

Centering matrix

$\bC=\bI_n-\tfrac1n\bJ_n$: symmetric, idempotent, rank $n-1$, $\bC\bone=\bzero$, $\sum(Y_i-\bar Y)^2=\bY^\top\bC\bY$, $\bJ^2=n\bJ$.

PSD sums

$\bB^\top\bB\succeq0$; $\bK\succ0$ ⇒ $\bM^\top\bK\bM\succeq0$. Diagonal entries of a PSD matrix are $\ge0$.

Projections Appendix A.6L4

A square $\bP$ is idempotent if $\bP^2=\bP$; a symmetric idempotent matrix is a projection.

  • (A.6.1) $\bP$ symmetric: idempotent of rank $r$ ⇔ $r$ eigenvalues equal to 1 and $n-r$ equal to 0.
  • (A.6.2) Projection ⇒ $\tr\bP=\rank\bP$.
  • (A.6.3) $\bP$ idempotent ⇒ $\bI_n-\bP$ idempotent.
  • (A.6.4) Projections are PSD.
  • (A.6.5) $\bP_1,\bP_2$ projections with $\bP_1-\bP_2\succeq0$ ⇒ $\bP_1\bP_2=\bP_2\bP_1=\bP_2$ and $\bP_1-\bP_2$ is a projection.
Subspace projections Appendix B.3L12 reference
  • (B.3.2) $W\subset\Omega$ ⇒ $\bP_\Omega-\bP_W=\bP_{W^\perp\cap\Omega}$.
  • (B.3.3) $W=\mathrm{Null}(\bM)\cap\Omega$ ⇒ $W^\perp\cap\Omega=\mathcal C(\bP_\Omega\bM^\top)$.
  • If $\bB$ is $n\times q$ of rank $q$, the projection onto $\mathcal C(\bB)$ is $\bB(\bB^\top\bB)^{-1}\bB^\top$.
Partitioned inverse A.9L4 reference $$\begin{pmatrix}\bA_{11}&\bA_{12}\\ \bA_{21}&\bA_{22}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}(\bA_{11}-\bA_{12}\bA_{22}^{-1}\bA_{21})^{-1} & -\bA_{11}^{-1}\bA_{12}(\bA_{22}-\bA_{21}\bA_{11}^{-1}\bA_{12})^{-1}\\ -\bA_{22}^{-1}\bA_{21}(\bA_{11}-\bA_{12}\bA_{22}^{-1}\bA_{21})^{-1} & (\bA_{22}-\bA_{21}\bA_{11}^{-1}\bA_{12})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$$

49.1L1Expectation and covariance operators

Definitions 1.1–1.3

$\E(\bZ)=(\E Z_{ij})$; $\Cov(\bX,\bY)=(\Cov(X_i,Y_j))$; $\Var(\bX)=\Cov(\bX,\bX)$.

Theorem 1.1 linearity

$\E(\bA\bZ\bB+\bC)=\bA\E(\bZ)\bB+\bC$. Corollary: $\E(\bA\bX+\bC)=\bA\E(\bX)+\bC$.

Theorem 1.2

If $\E\bX=\ba$, $\E\bY=\bb$: $\ \Cov(\bX,\bY)=\E[(\bX-\ba)(\bY-\bb)^\top]=\E(\bX\bY^\top)-\ba\bb^\top$. Corollary: $\Var(\bX)=\E(\bX\bX^\top)-\ba\ba^\top$.

Theorem 1.3 bilinearity

$\Cov(\bA\bX,\bB\bY)=\bA\Cov(\bX,\bY)\bB^\top$. Corollary: $\Var(\bA\bX)=\bA\Var(\bX)\bA^\top$.

Proof

$\bA\bX-\E(\bA\bX)=\bA(\bX-\ba)$, so $\Cov(\bA\bX,\bB\bY)=\E[\bA(\bX-\ba)(\bY-\bb)^\top\bB^\top]=\bA\Cov(\bX,\bY)\bB^\top$ by Theorem 1.1.

Theorem 1.4 variance matrices are PSD

$\Var(\bX)$ is non-negative definite. If no element of $\bX$ is a linear combination of the others (no $\ba\ne\bzero$, $b$ with $\ba^\top\bX=b$), then $\Var(\bX)$ is positive definite.

Proof (lecture)

For any $\bc$: $0\le\Var(\bc^\top\bX)=\bc^\top\Var(\bX)\bc$. Equality iff $\bc=\bzero$ or $\bc^\top\bX=b$ for some constant $b$; the second is ruled out, so $\bc^\top\Var(\bX)\bc>0$ for $\bc\ne\bzero$.

Shift identity HW 1a.1 $$\E[(\bX-\ba)(\bX-\ba)^\top]=\Var(\bX)+(\E\bX-\ba)(\E\bX-\ba)^\top,\qquad \E\|\bX-\ba\|^2=\textstyle\sum_i\sigma_{ii}+\|\E\bX-\ba\|^2.$$

Add and subtract $\E\bX$; cross terms vanish because $\E[\bX-\E\bX]=\bzero$.

49.2L1–2Mean and variance of quadratic forms

Theorem 1.5 mean of a quadratic form

$\E\bX=\bmu$, $\Var\bX=\Sig$, $\bA$ symmetric:

$$\E(\bX^\top\bA\bX)=\tr(\bA\Sig)+\bmu^\top\bA\bmu.$$

Corollary: $\E[(\bX-\bb)^\top\bA(\bX-\bb)]=\tr(\bA\Sig)+(\bmu-\bb)^\top\bA(\bmu-\bb)$. If $\Sig=\sigma^2\bI_n$, then $\tr(\bA\Sig)=\sigma^2\tr\bA$.

Proof 1 (entrywise, lecture)

$\E[(\bX-\bmu)^\top\bA(\bX-\bmu)]=\E(\bX^\top\bA\bX)-\bmu^\top\bA\bmu$. Also $\E[(\bX-\bmu)^\top\bA(\bX-\bmu)]=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\sigma_{ij}=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\sigma_{ji}=\sum_i(\bA\Sig)_{ii}=\tr(\bA\Sig)$, using symmetry of $\Sig$.

Proof 2 (trace trick, lecture)

$\E(\bX^\top\bA\bX)=\E[\tr(\bA\bX\bX^\top)]=\tr[\bA\E(\bX\bX^\top)]=\tr[\bA(\Sig+\bmu\bmu^\top)]=\tr(\bA\Sig)+\bmu^\top\bA\bmu$.

Lecture 2 examples

Successive differences. $X_i$ iid $(\mu,\sigma^2)$, $Q=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(X_i-X_{i+1})^2$. $Q$ does not depend on $\mu$, so take $\mu=0$. $Q=\bX^\top\bA\bX$ with $\bA$ tridiagonal: diagonal $(1,2,\dots,2,1)$, off-diagonal $-1$. Then $\E Q=\sigma^2\tr\bA=(2n-2)\sigma^2$.

Equicorrelation. $\Var\bX=\sigma^2[(1-\rho)\bI+\rho\bJ]$, $Q=\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2=\bX^\top\bC\bX$. Since $\bC\bJ=\bzero$, $\bC\Sig=\sigma^2(1-\rho)\bC$, so $\E Q=\sigma^2(1-\rho)(n-1)$.

Trick used in both: if $Q$ is invariant to shifting all $X_i$ by $\mu$, set $\mu=0$ so the $\bmu^\top\bA\bmu$ term disappears (formally, $\bA\bone=\bzero$).

Theorem 1.6 variance of a quadratic form

$X_1,\dots,X_n$ independent, means $\theta_i$, common central moments $\mu_2,\mu_3,\mu_4$; $\bA$ symmetric, $\ba=$ diagonal of $\bA$:

$$\Var(\bX^\top\bA\bX)=(\mu_4-3\mu_2^2)\,\ba^\top\ba+2\mu_2^2\tr(\bA^2)+4\mu_2\,\boldsymbol\theta^\top\bA^2\boldsymbol\theta+4\mu_3\,\boldsymbol\theta^\top\bA\ba.$$

Normal case ($\mu_3=0$, $\mu_4=3\mu_2^2$), $\bZ\sim N(\bzero,\mu_2\bI)$: $\ \Var(\bZ^\top\bA\bZ)=2\mu_2^2\tr(\bA^2)$.

How HW 1b.5 uses it

For $(n-1)S^2=\bX^\top\bC\bX$ with $\boldsymbol\theta=\mu\bone$: $\bC\boldsymbol\theta=\bzero$ kills the last two terms, normality kills the first, and $\tr\bC^2=\tr\bC=n-1$. So $\Var S^2=2\sigma^4/(n-1)$. Always check whether $\bA\boldsymbol\theta=\bzero$ first.

Covariance of quadratic forms HW Misc 1.5

$\bX\sim N(\bzero,\sigma^2\bI)$: $\ \Cov(\bX^\top\bA\bX,\bX^\top\bB\bX)=2\sigma^4\tr(\bA\bB)$.

Proof: polarization, $\Cov(U,V)=\tfrac12[\Var(U+V)-\Var U-\Var V]$, then Theorem 1.6 on $\bA+\bB$, $\bA$, $\bB$.

49.3L2Independence and moment generating functions

Independence

$\bX\perp\bY$ iff $f_{\bX,\bY}(\bx,\mathbf y)=f_{\bX}(\bx)f_{\bY}(\mathbf y)$ (or the CDFs factor) for all $\bx,\mathbf y$.

Propositions
  • $\bX\perp\bY$ ⇒ $\Cov(\bX,\bY)=\bzero$. Proof: the double integral of $xyf_X(x)f_Y(y)$ factors into $\E X\cdot\E Y$.
  • $\bX\perp\bY$ ⇒ $\E[g(\bX)h(\bY)]=\E g(\bX)\,\E h(\bY)$.
  • Uncorrelated does not imply independent: $X\sim U(-1,1)$, $Y=X^2$ has $\Cov(X,Y)=\E X^3-\E X\,\E X^2=0$.
MGF and characteristic function

$M_{\bX}(\bt)=\E e^{\bt^\top\bX}$, $\varphi_{\bX}(\bt)=\E e^{i\bt^\top\bX}$. The CF always exists and determines the distribution; the MGF determines it when it exists near $\bzero$.

Property 1.1

$\bX\perp\bY$ (same dimension) ⇒ $M_{\bX+\bY}(\bt)=M_{\bX}(\bt)M_{\bY}(\bt)$.

Property 1.2 MGF test for independence

$\bX\perp\bY$ ⇔ $M_{\bX,\bY}(\bt_1,\bt_2)=M_{\bX}(\bt_1)M_{\bY}(\bt_2)$, where $M_{\bX,\bY}(\bt_1,\bt_2)=\E e^{\bt_1^\top\bX+\bt_2^\top\bY}$.

χ² subtraction lemma Lecture 2, used in Thm 3.5

$M_{\chi^2_r}(t)=(1-2t)^{-r/2}$ for $t\lt\tfrac12$. If $Q_1\sim\chi^2_{r_1}$, $Q_2\sim\chi^2_{r_2}$ ($r_1>r_2$), and $Q=Q_1-Q_2$ is independent of $Q_2$, then $Q\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$.

Proof: $M_{Q_1}(t)=\E e^{tQ+tQ_2}=\E e^{tQ}\,\E e^{tQ_2}$, so $\E e^{tQ}=(1-2t)^{-r_1/2}/(1-2t)^{-r_2/2}=(1-2t)^{-(r_1-r_2)/2}$.

50.1L3Definitions

Definition 2.1 density

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$, $\Sig$ positive definite, if

$$f(\mathbf y)=k^{-1}\exp\!\left\{-\tfrac12(\mathbf y-\bmu)^\top\Sig^{-1}(\mathbf y-\bmu)\right\},\qquad k=(2\pi)^{n/2}\det(\Sig)^{1/2}.$$
Theorem 2.1

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ ⇒ $\E\bY=\bmu$, $\Var\bY=\Sig$.

Property 2.1 and 2.2 standardizing

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ ⇒ $\bZ=\Sig^{-1/2}(\bY-\bmu)\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$. Conversely $\bZ\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$ ⇒ $\Sig^{1/2}\bZ+\bmu\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$. Proof: Jacobian change of variables.

Definition 2.2 allows singular Σ

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ if $\bY\overset d=\bA\bZ+\bmu$ with $\bZ\sim N_m(\bzero,\bI_m)$, $\bA$ $n\times m$, $\Sig=\bA\bA^\top$. If $\rank\Sig=m\lt n$, the distribution is singular. For $\Sig$ PD, Definitions 2.1 and 2.2 agree (one direction by Theorem 2.2 with $\bZ=\bA^{-1}(\bY-\bmu)$; the other by matching MGFs).

Proposition MGF $$M_{\bY}(\bt)=\exp\!\left(\bt^\top\bmu+\tfrac12\bt^\top\Sig\bt\right).$$
Proof (lecture)

$M_{\bZ}(\bt)=\prod_i\E e^{t_iZ_i}=\prod_ie^{t_i^2/2}=e^{\bt^\top\bt/2}$. Theorem 2.2 linear transformations

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$, $\bC$ is $m\times n$ of rank $m$ ($m\le n$), $\mathbf d$ is $m\times1$:

$$\bC\bY+\mathbf d\sim N_m(\bC\bmu+\mathbf d,\ \bC\Sig\bC^\top).$$

Proof: with $\mathbf s=\bC^\top\bt$, $\E e^{\bt^\top(\bC\bY+\mathbf d)}=e^{\bt^\top\mathbf d}M_{\bY}(\mathbf s)=\exp(\tfrac12\bt^\top\bC\Sig\bC^\top\bt+\bt^\top\bC\bmu+\bt^\top\mathbf d)$. Full row rank keeps $\bC\Sig\bC^\top$ positive definite.

Corollaries and facts
  • $\bZ\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI)$, $\bA$ nonsingular ⇒ $\bA\bZ+\bmu\sim N_n(\bmu,\bA\bA^\top)$.
  • $\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\sigma^2\bI)$, $\bP$ orthogonal ⇒ $\bP\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\sigma^2\bI)$.
  • $\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\sigma^2\bI)$ ⇒ the $Y_i\sim N(\mu_i,\sigma^2)$ are independent (density factors).
Lecture 3 example Ȳ ⊥ Q via an orthogonal (Helmert) matrix

$\bY\sim N_n(\mu\bone,\sigma^2\bI)$, $Q=\tfrac1{\sigma^2}\sum(Y_i-\bar Y)^2$. Let $Z_i=(Y_i-\mu)/\sigma$, so $Q=\sum Z_i^2-n\bar Z^2$. Take orthogonal $\bP$ with first row $\bone^\top/\sqrt n$ and $\bX=\bP\bZ\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI)$. Then $X_1=\sqrt n\bar Z$ and $\sum X_i^2=\bZ^\top\bP^\top\bP\bZ=\sum Z_i^2$, so $Q=\sum_{i=2}^nX_i^2\sim\chi^2_{n-1}$. $\bar Y$ depends only on $X_1$, which is independent of $X_2,\dots,X_n$.

$\bY$ with mean $\bmu$, variance $\Sig$: $\ \bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$ ⇔ $\ba^\top\bY$ is univariate normal for every $\ba\in\mathbb R^n$.

Proof (lecture)

"If": $\ba^\top\bY\sim N(\ba^\top\bmu,\ba^\top\Sig\ba)$, so $\E e^{t\ba^\top\bY}=\exp(t\ba^\top\bmu+\tfrac12t^2\ba^\top\Sig\ba)$. Set $t=1$: this is $M_{\bY}(\ba)$ for $N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$. "Only if": by Definition 2.2, $\ba^\top\bY=(\ba^\top\bA)\bZ+\ba^\top\bmu$, a normal.

Theorem 2.4 zero covariance ⇔ independence

$\bY=(\bY_1^\top,\bY_2^\top)^\top\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$: $\ \bY_1\perp\bY_2$ ⇔ $\Sig_{12}=\bzero$.

Conversely, factoring for all $\bt_1,\bt_2$ forces $\bt_1^\top\Sig_{12}\bt_2=0$, so $\Sig_{12}=\bzero$.

Theorem 2.5 independence of linear forms

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$: $\ \bA\bY\perp\bB\bY$ ⇔ $\Cov(\bA\bY,\bB\bY)=\bA\Sig\bB^\top=\bzero$.

Proof: $\bZ=\binom{\bA}{\bB}\bY$ is MVN (Theorem 2.2); apply Theorem 2.4.

Lecture 4 example

$\bar Y=\tfrac1n\bone^\top\bY$ and $\bU=(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)\bY$: $\ \Cov(\bar Y,\bU)=\tfrac1n\bone^\top\sigma^2\bI(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)=\tfrac{\sigma^2}{n}(\bone^\top-\bone^\top)=\bzero$. So $\bar Y\perp\bU$, and $Q=\bU^\top\bU/\sigma^2$ is a function of $\bU$.

Conditional distribution Example 2.9

$$\bY_1\mid\bY_2=\mathbf y_2\ \sim\ N_p\!\left(\bmu_1+\Sig_{12}\Sig_{22}^{-1}(\mathbf y_2-\bmu_2),\ \ \Sig_{11}-\Sig_{12}\Sig_{22}^{-1}\Sig_{21}\right).$$

50.4L5Distribution of quadratic forms

Theorem 2.7 χ² ⇔ idempotent

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$, $\bA$ symmetric. Then $Q=\bY^\top\bA\bY\sim\chi^2_r$ iff $\bA$ is idempotent of rank $r$.

Proof (lecture)

"If": $\bA=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda\mathbf T^\top$, $\mathbf T$ orthogonal, $\boldsymbol\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(1,\dots,1,0,\dots,0)$ with $r$ ones (A.6.1). $\bZ=\mathbf T^\top\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$, so $Q=\bZ^\top\boldsymbol\Lambda\bZ=\sum_{i=1}^rZ_i^2\sim\chi^2_r$.

"Only if": write $\bA=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda\mathbf T^\top$ with general eigenvalues $\lambda_i$, so $Q=\sum\lambda_iZ_i^2$ and $\E e^{tQ}=\prod_i(1-2t\lambda_i)^{-1/2}$. Matching $(1-2t)^{-r/2}$ forces $r$ of the $\lambda_i$ to be 1 and the rest 0. Then $\boldsymbol\Lambda^2=\boldsymbol\Lambda$, so $\bA^2=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda^2\mathbf T^\top=\bA$.

Corollary

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$, $\bA$ symmetric, $\bY^\top\bA\bY\sim\chi^2_r$ ⇒ $\bY^\top(\bI_n-\bA)\bY\sim\chi^2_{n-r}$ (A.6.3).

Scaling to σ²

Theorem 2.7 is stated for $N(\bzero,\bI)$. For $\bY\sim N(\mu\bone,\sigma^2\bI)$ or $N(\bX\bbeta,\sigma^2\bI)$, first standardize: $\bZ=(\bY-\bmu)/\sigma$, and check that $\bA\bmu=\bzero$ so the quadratic form in $\bY$ equals $\sigma^2\bZ^\top\bA\bZ$. The lecture examples always do this step.

Lecture 5 example (n−1)S²/σ² ∼ χ²ₙ₋₁

$\bZ=(\bY-\mu\bone)/\sigma\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI)$ and $(\bY-\bar\bY)/\sigma=(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)\bZ$. So $(n-1)S^2/\sigma^2=\bZ^\top(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)^\top(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)\bZ=\bZ^\top(\bI-\tfrac1n\bJ)\bZ$. The matrix is symmetric idempotent with trace $n-1$; Theorem 2.7.

Lecture 5 example independence of two χ² forms

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI_n)$, $\bA,\bB$ symmetric, $\bY^\top\bA\bY$ and $\bY^\top\bB\bY$ both χ². Then they are independent iff $\bA\bB=\bzero$.

Proof (lecture)

"If": $\bA\bB=\bzero$ ⇒ $\bA\bY\perp\bB\bY$ (Theorem 2.5). Theorem 2.7 makes $\bA,\bB$ idempotent, so $\bY^\top\bA\bY=\bY^\top\bA^\top\bA\bY=\|\bA\bY\|^2$ and $\bY^\top\bB\bY=\|\bB\bY\|^2$, functions of independent vectors.

"Only if": the sum of independent χ²'s is χ², so $\bY^\top(\bA+\bB)\bY$ is χ² and $\bA+\bB$ is idempotent. Expanding gives $\bA\bB+\bB\bA=\bzero$. Multiply by $\bA$ on the left: $\bA\bB+\bA\bB\bA=\bzero$; on the right: $\bA\bB\bA+\bB\bA=\bzero$. Hence $\bA\bB=\bB\bA$, and then $\bA\bB=\bB\bA=\bzero$.

Lecture 5 example difference of χ² forms

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI)$, $Q_i=\bY^\top\bP_i\bY\sim\chi^2_{r_i}$ with $\bP_i$ symmetric, and $Q_1-Q_2\ge0$. Then $Q_1-Q_2\sim\chi^2_{r_1-r_2}$, independent of $Q_2$.

Proof: Theorem 2.7 makes $\bP_1,\bP_2$ projections; $Q_1-Q_2=\bY^\top(\bP_1-\bP_2)\bY\ge0$ means $\bP_1-\bP_2\succeq0$. By A.6.5 it is a projection of rank $r_1-r_2$, so Theorem 2.7 gives the χ². Also $\bP_1\bP_2=\bP_2$, so $(\bP_1-\bP_2)\bP_2=\bzero$, and the previous example gives independence. (This is the structure behind $\mathrm{RSS}_H-\mathrm{RSS}$.)

Theorem 2.8 general Σ

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\Sig)$, $\bA$ symmetric. Then $\bY^\top\bA\bY\sim\chi^2_r$ iff $r$ eigenvalues of $\bA\Sig$ are 1 and the other $n-r$ are 0.

Proof (lecture)

$\Sig=\bR\bR^\top$ with $\bR$ $n\times m$, so $\bY=\bR\bZ$, $\bZ\sim N_m(\bzero,\bI_m)$ (Definition 2.2) and $Q=\bZ^\top(\bR^\top\bA\bR)\bZ$. By Theorem 2.7, $Q\sim\chi^2_r$ iff $\bR^\top\bA\bR$ is idempotent of rank $r$. $\bR^\top\bA\bR$ and $\bA\bR\bR^\top=\bA\Sig$ share nonzero eigenvalues, and $\tr(\bA\Sig)=\tr(\bR^\top\bA\bR)=r$, so $\bA\Sig$ has $r$ unit eigenvalues and the rest zero.

Corollary Σ positive definite

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\Sig)$, $\Sig\succ0$, $\bA$ symmetric: $\bY^\top\bA\bY\sim\chi^2_r$ iff $\bA\Sig$ is idempotent of rank $r$.

Proof: $\bR$ is invertible; conjugate $(\bR^\top\bA\bR)^2=\bR^\top\bA\bR$ by $(\bR^\top)^{-1}(\cdot)\bR^\top$ to get $(\bA\Sig)^2=\bA\Sig$.

How HW 2d.4 is graded

Write the statistic as $\bY^\top\bA\bY$, display $\bA$, verify $\bA^2=\bA$ and $\rank\bA=\tr\bA$. For $n=3$: $\tfrac13\sum_{\text{cyc}}(Y_i-Y_{i+1})^2=\bY^\top(\bI-\tfrac13\bJ)\bY\sim\chi^2_2$. For $n\ge4$ no multiple of the cyclic sum is idempotent.

Theorem 2.9 Mahalanobis form

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\Sig)$, $\Sig$ PD ⇒ $Q=(\bY-\bmu)^\top\Sig^{-1}(\bY-\bmu)\sim\chi^2_n$.

Proof (lecture): $\bY=\Sig^{1/2}\bZ+\bmu$, so $Q=\bZ^\top\Sig^{1/2}\Sig^{-1}\Sig^{1/2}\bZ=\bZ^\top\bZ=\sum Z_i^2$.

Linear vs quadratic textbook, follows from Thm 2.5

$\bY\sim N_n(\bmu,\sigma^2\bI)$, $\bA$ a projection: $\bY^\top\bA\bY\perp\bB\bY$ if $\bB\bA=\bzero$. Proof: $\bY^\top\bA\bY=\|\bA\bY\|^2$ and $\Cov(\bB\bY,\bA\bY)=\sigma^2\bB\bA=\bzero$; Theorem 2.5. With a nonzero mean the form is noncentral χ², which the lectures do not cover.

Three-way split HW Misc 2.8

$\bY\sim N_n(\bzero,\bI)$, $\bA,\bB$ symmetric idempotent with $\bA\bB=\bB\bA=\bzero$. Then $\bY^\top\bA\bY$, $\bY^\top\bB\bY$, $\bY^\top(\bI-\bA-\bB)\bY$ are independent χ².

51.1L6Least squares estimation

Normal equations

Minimizing $\beps^\top\beps$ makes $\bY-\bX\bhat\perp\Omega=\mathcal C(\bX)$, i.e. $\bX^\top(\bY-\bX\bhat)=\bzero$:

$$\bX^\top\bX\bhat=\bX^\top\bY,\qquad \bhat=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bY.$$

Calculus route: $\tfrac{d}{d\bbeta}(\bbeta^\top\ba)=\ba$, $\tfrac{d}{d\bbeta}(\bbeta^\top\bA\bbeta)=(\bA+\bA^\top)\bbeta$, so $\tfrac{d}{d\bbeta}\beps^\top\beps=-2\bX^\top\bY+2\bX^\top\bX\bbeta$.

Why β̂ minimizes (lecture)

Write $\bY-\bX\bbeta=(\bY-\bX\bhat)+(\bX\bhat-\bX\bbeta)$. With $\bP=\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top$ the cross term is $\bY^\top(\bI-\bP)\bP(\bY-\bX\bbeta)=0$ since $\bP^2=\bP$. So $\beps^\top\beps=\|\bY-\bX\bhat\|^2+\|\bX\bhat-\bX\bbeta\|^2\ge\|\bY-\bX\bhat\|^2$, with equality iff $\bbeta=\bhat$ (full rank).

Theorem 3.1 hat matrix

$\rank\bX=p$, $\bP=\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top$:

  1. $\bP$ and $\bI_n-\bP$ are symmetric and idempotent.
  2. $\rank(\bI_n-\bP)=\tr(\bI_n-\bP)=n-p$.
  3. $\bP\bX=\bX$, $\bP$ projects onto $\Omega=\mathcal C(\bX)$; $(\bI_n-\bP)\bX=\bzero$, so $\bI_n-\bP$ projects onto $\Omega^\perp$.
Proof

(i) $\bP^2=\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top=\bP$; A.6.3 for $\bI-\bP$. (ii) $\tr\bP=\tr((\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bX)=\tr\bI_p=p$; A.6.2. (iii) direct multiplication.

Consequences
  • $\hat\bY=\bP\bY$, $\be=(\bI-\bP)\bY$, $\bX^\top\be=\bzero$, $\hat\bY^\top\be=0$.
  • With an intercept column $\bone_n$: $\sum_i(Y_i-\hat Y_i)=\bone^\top\be=0$ (HW 3a.2).
  • $\mathrm{RSS}=\be^\top\be=\bY^\top(\bI-\bP)\bY=\bY^\top\bY-\bhat^\top\bX^\top\bX\bhat$.
  • $\bY=\bone\beta_0+\beps$ ⇒ $\hat\beta_0=(\bone^\top\bone)^{-1}\bone^\top\bY=\bar Y$.

51.2L7–8Properties of β̂ and Gauss–Markov

RSS expressions Lecture 7

(1) $\be^\top\be=\bY^\top\bY-\bhat^\top\bX^\top\bY$; (2) $\be^\top\be=\bY^\top\bY-\bhat^\top\bX^\top\bX\bhat=\|\bY\|^2-\|\bX\bhat\|^2$; (3) $\be^\top\be=\bY^\top(\bI_n-\bP)\bY$.

Proof of (1)–(2): expand $(\bY-\bX\bhat)^\top(\bY-\bX\bhat)=\bY^\top\bY-\bY^\top\bX\bhat-\bhat^\top(\bX^\top\bY-\bX^\top\bX\bhat)$; the bracket is $\bzero$ by the normal equations, and $\bY^\top\bX\bhat=\bhat^\top\bX^\top\bY=\bhat^\top\bX^\top\bX\bhat$ by them again. (3) uses $(\bI-\bP)^\top(\bI-\bP)=\bI-\bP$.

Moments of β̂ Lecture 7

$\E\beps=\bzero$, $\rank\bX=p$ ⇒ $\E\bhat=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bX\bbeta=\bbeta$.

Uncorrelated errors with common variance ⇒ $\Var\bhat=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\sigma^2\bI_n\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}=\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}$. Also $\Var\be=\sigma^2(\bI-\bP)$ and $\Cov(\bhat,\be)=\bzero$.

X not of full rank Lecture 7 reference

If $\rank\bX\lt p$, the normal equations have many solutions; one is $\bhat=(\bX^\top\bX)^-\bX^\top\bY$ with $\bB\bB^-\bB=\bB$. Theorem 3.1 holds with $p$ replaced by $\rank\bX$, and $\bP=\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^-\bX^\top$ does not depend on the choice of g-inverse, so $\bX\bhat=\bP\bY$ is unique.

No linear unbiased estimator of β. If $\bA\bY$ were unbiased, $\bA\bX\bbeta=\bbeta$ for all $\bbeta$ forces $\bA\bX=\bI_p$, but $p=\rank(\bA\bX)\le\rank\bX\lt p$, a contradiction.

Estimable function Lecture 7, §3.9.2

$\bc^\top\bbeta$ is estimable if some $\ba^\top\bY$ is unbiased for $\bc^\top\bbeta$ for all $\bbeta$.

  • $\bc^\top\bbeta$ estimable ⇔ $\bc\in\mathcal C(\bX^\top)$. Proof: $\ba^\top\bX\bbeta=\bc^\top\bbeta$ for all $\bbeta$ ⇔ $\bc=\bX^\top\ba$.
  • If estimable, $\bc^\top\bhat=\ba^\top\bX\bhat=\ba^\top\bP\bY$ is the same for every solution of the normal equations.
  • $\bc^\top\bbeta$ estimable for all $\bc$ ⇔ $\mathcal C(\bX^\top)=\mathbb R^p$ ⇔ $\rank\bX=p$ (Lecture 8).
Straight line Lecture 9, HW 3b.1

$\bX^\top\bX=\begin{pmatrix}n&n\bar x\\ n\bar x&\sum x_i^2\end{pmatrix}$, $\det=nS_{xx}$, $\ (\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}=\dfrac1{nS_{xx}}\begin{pmatrix}\sum x_i^2&-n\bar x\\-n\bar x&n\end{pmatrix}$.

$$\hat\beta_1=\frac{\sum(x_i-\bar x)(Y_i-\bar Y)}{\sum(x_i-\bar x)^2},\quad \hat\beta_0=\bar Y-\hat\beta_1\bar x,\quad \Var\hat\beta_1=\frac{\sigma^2}{S_{xx}},\quad \Cov(\hat\beta_0,\hat\beta_1)=-\frac{\sigma^2\bar x}{S_{xx}}.$$
Theorem 3.2 Gauss–Markov

Assume $\E\beps=\bzero$, $\Var\varepsilon_i=\sigma^2$, $\Cov(\varepsilon_i,\varepsilon_j)=0$ ($i\ne j$). If $\bc^\top\bbeta$ is estimable, then the (unique) $\bc^\top\bhat$ has minimum variance among all linear unbiased estimators: it is the BLUE.

Proof (lecture)

Let $\ba^\top\bY$ be unbiased for $\bc^\top\bbeta$, so $\bc=\bX^\top\ba$. Let $\bP$ be the projection onto $\mathcal C(\bX)$ and split $\ba^\top\bY=\ba^\top\bP\bY+\ba^\top(\bI-\bP)\bY$. Since $\Cov(\ba^\top\bP\bY,\ba^\top(\bI-\bP)\bY)=\sigma^2\ba^\top\bP(\bI-\bP)\ba=0$,

$\Var(\ba^\top\bY)=\Var(\ba^\top\bP\bY)+\Var(\ba^\top(\bI-\bP)\bY)\ge\Var(\ba^\top\bP\bY)=\Var(\ba^\top\bX\bhat)=\Var(\bc^\top\bhat)$.

Notes on Gauss–Markov Lecture 8
  • No distributional assumption on the $\varepsilon_i$ is needed. Under normality, $\bc^\top\bhat$ has minimum variance among all unbiased estimators, not just linear ones.
  • $\E(\bc^\top\bhat)=\bc^\top\bbeta$ for estimable $\bc^\top\bbeta$ does not give $\E\bhat=\bbeta$ when $\rank\bX\lt p$: then $\E\bhat=(\bX^\top\bX)^-\bX^\top\bX\bbeta$ depends on the g-inverse.
  • Corollary: if $\rank\bX=p$, $\bc^\top\bhat$ is the BLUE for every $\bc\in\mathbb R^p$.
Consistency Lecture 8 proposition

$\varepsilon_i$ iid $(0,\sigma^2)$. If the smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_p(n)$ of $\bX^\top\bX$ tends to $\infty$, then $\bhat\xrightarrow{P}\bbeta$.

Proof (lecture)

Eventually $\lambda_p(n)\gt0$, so $\bhat$ is unique. The largest eigenvalue of $(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}$ is $1/\lambda_p(n)$, so for any $\ba$: $\Var(\ba^\top\bhat)=\sigma^2\ba^\top(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\ba\le\sigma^2\ba^\top\ba/\lambda_p(n)\to0$. Chebyshev (Markov on the square): $P(|\ba^\top\bhat-\ba^\top\bbeta|\ge\varepsilon)\le\Var(\ba^\top\bhat)/\varepsilon^2\to0$. Take $\ba=\mathbf e_i$.

Same idea in HW 3b.3

Minimum-variance linear unbiased estimate of $\theta$ from a $N(\theta,\sigma^2)$ sample: write $\ba=\bone/n+\mathbf d$; unbiasedness forces $\mathbf d^\top\bone=0$, so $\ba^\top\ba=\tfrac1n+\mathbf d^\top\mathbf d\ge\tfrac1n$. Answer $\bar Y$. (The official solution just cites Gauss–Markov with $\bX=\bone$.)

51.3L8Estimating σ²

Theorem 3.3 S² is unbiased

$\E\beps=\bzero$, $\Var\beps=\sigma^2\bI_n$, $\rank\bX=p$ ⇒ $\E S^2=\sigma^2$, where $S^2=\dfrac{\mathrm{RSS}}{n-p}=\dfrac{(\bY-\bX\bhat)^\top(\bY-\bX\bhat)}{n-p}$.

Proof (lecture): $\mathrm{RSS}=\bY^\top(\bI-\bP)\bY$ with $\E\bY=\bX\bbeta$, $\Var\bY=\sigma^2\bI$. Theorem 1.5: $\E\,\mathrm{RSS}=\tr((\bI-\bP)\sigma^2\bI)+(\bX\bbeta)^\top(\bI-\bP)\bX\bbeta=\sigma^2(n-p)$.

Theorem 3.4 optimality of S²

$Y_1,\dots,Y_n$ independent with common variance $\sigma^2$ and common third and fourth central moments $\mu_3,\mu_4$; $\E\bY=\bX\bbeta$, $\rank\bX=p$. Then $S^2$ is the unique nonnegative quadratic unbiased estimator of $\sigma^2$ with minimum variance when $\mu_4=3\sigma^4$ or when the diagonal elements of $\bP$ are all equal.

Proof idea (lecture): use the formulas for $\E(\bY^\top\bA\bY)$ and $\Var(\bY^\top\bA\bY)$ (Theorems 1.5, 1.6); HW 3c.2 carries this out for $\bX=\bone_n$. Under normality $S^2$ is minimum-variance among all unbiased estimators, not only quadratic ones.

Variance of S²

Under normality $\Var S^2=2\sigma^4/(n-p)$ (since $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$). In general, Theorem 1.6 with $\bA=\bI-\bP$, $\bA\bX\bbeta=\bzero$ gives $\Var\,\mathrm{RSS}=2\sigma^4(n-p)+(\mu_4-3\sigma^4)\sum_i(1-p_{ii})^2$.

51.4L9Distribution theory and maximum likelihood

Theorem 3.5

If $\bY\sim N_n(\bX\bbeta,\sigma^2\bI_n)$ and $\rank\bX=p$:

  1. $\bhat\sim N_p(\bbeta,\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1})$;
  2. $(\bhat-\bbeta)^\top\bX^\top\bX(\bhat-\bbeta)/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_p$;
  3. $\bhat$ is independent of $S^2=\be^\top\be/(n-p)$;
  4. $\mathrm{RSS}/\sigma^2=(n-p)S^2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$.
Proof (lecture)

(i) $\bhat=\bC\bY$; Theorem 2.2. (ii) $\Var(\bhat)^{-1}=\bX^\top\bX/\sigma^2$; Theorem 2.9. (iii) $\bhat$ and $\be=(\bI-\bP)\bY$ are jointly normal with $\Cov(\bhat,\be)=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\sigma^2\bI(\bI-\bP)=\bzero$ because $\bX^\top=\bX^\top\bP$; Theorem 2.5.

(iv) Method 1: $(\bI-\bP)\bX\bbeta=\bzero$, so $\mathrm{RSS}=\beps^\top(\bI-\bP)\beps$ with $\bI-\bP$ a projection of rank $n-p$; Theorem 2.7. Method 2: $Q_1=\beps^\top\beps=\underbrace{\|\bY-\bX\bhat\|^2}_{Q}+\underbrace{\|\bX\bhat-\bX\bbeta\|^2}_{Q_2}$ with $Q_1/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_n$, $Q_2/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_p$ by (ii), $Q\perp Q_2$ by (iii); the χ² subtraction lemma (Lecture 2) gives $Q/\sigma^2\sim\chi^2_{n-p}$.

Remarks t statistics

$\hat\beta_j\sim N(\beta_j,\sigma^2[(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}]_{j+1,j+1})$ and

$$\frac{\hat\beta_j-\beta_j}{S\sqrt{[(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}]_{j+1,j+1}}}=\frac{(\hat\beta_j-\beta_j)/\big(\sigma\sqrt{[\cdot]_{j+1,j+1}}\big)}{\sqrt{\frac{(n-p)S^2/\sigma^2}{n-p}}}\sim t_{n-p}.$$

Construction: $Z\sim N(0,1)\perp U\sim\chi^2_n$ ⇒ $Z/\sqrt{U/n}\sim t_n$. Independence of numerator and denominator is Theorem 3.5(iii).

Maximum likelihood

$\log L=-\tfrac n2\log2\pi-\tfrac n2\log\sigma^2-\tfrac1{2\sigma^2}(\mathbf y-\bX\bbeta)^\top(\mathbf y-\bX\bbeta)$. Setting both derivatives to zero:

$$\bhat_{\rm MLE}=(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bY=\bhat_{\rm LSE},\qquad \hat\sigma^2_{\rm MLE}=\frac{\mathrm{RSS}}{n}=\frac{(n-p)S^2}{n}.$$

$\E\hat\sigma^2_{\rm MLE}=\frac{n-p}{n}\sigma^2$ (biased, asymptotically unbiased). $\Var S^2=\frac{2\sigma^4}{n-p}$, $\Var\hat\sigma^2_{\rm MLE}=\frac{2(n-p)\sigma^4}{n^2}$.

51.5L10Orthogonal columns

Orthogonal design

$\bX=[\bx^0,\dots,\bx^{p-1}]$ with $(\bx^i)^\top\bx^j=0$ for $i\ne j$. Then $\bX^\top\bX=\mathrm{diag}((\bx^j)^\top\bx^j)$ and

$$\hat\beta_j=\frac{(\bx^j)^\top\bY}{(\bx^j)^\top\bx^j},\qquad \mathrm{RSS}=\bY^\top\bY-\sum_j\hat\beta_j^2\,(\bx^j)^\top\bx^j.$$
  • Dropping covariate $j$ leaves every other $\hat\beta_l$ unchanged (HW 3a.4).
  • Dropping it raises RSS by $\hat\beta_j^2(\bx^j)^\top\bx^j\ge0$.
  • The $\hat\beta_j$ are uncorrelated since $\Var\bhat$ is diagonal.
Lecture 10 example orthogonal designs are optimal

Centered covariates with $\sum_ix_{ij}=0$, $\sum_ix_{ij}^2=c$. Then $\bX^\top\bX=\mathrm{diag}(n,\bW)$ with $\bW$ having diagonal $c$. Write $\bW=\mathbf T\boldsymbol\Lambda\mathbf T^\top$:

$$\sum_j\Var\hat\beta_j=\sigma^2\tr(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}=\sigma^2\Big(\tfrac1n+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\tfrac1{\lambda_j}\Big),\quad\text{subject to }\sum\lambda_j=\tr\bW=c(p-1).$$

Lagrange multipliers give all $\lambda_j=c$, so $\bW=c\bI$: the columns are orthogonal.

51.6L10Centering and scaling

Centering

Rewrite $Y_i=\alpha_0+\sum_j\beta_j(x_{ij}-\bar x_j)+\varepsilon_i$ with $\alpha_0=\beta_0+\sum_j\beta_j\bar x_j$, design $\bX_c=[\bone_n,\tilde\bX]$. Since $\bone^\top\tilde\bX=\bzero$:

$$\hat\alpha_0=\bar Y,\qquad \hat\bbeta_{1:p-1}=(\tilde\bX^\top\tilde\bX)^{-1}\tilde\bX^\top\bY\ \ (\text{unchanged slopes}).$$

51.8L11–12Estimation with linear restrictions

$H:\bA\bbeta=\bc$, $\bA$ is $q\times p$ of rank $q$, $\bc$ known.

Restricted LSE Lagrange multipliers $$\bhat_H=\bhat+(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bA^\top\big[\bA(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bA^\top\big]^{-1}(\bc-\bA\bhat).$$
Derivation (lecture)

Minimize $\gamma=(\bY-\bX\bbeta)^\top(\bY-\bX\bbeta)+\blam^\top(\bA\bbeta-\bc)$: $\ \partial\gamma/\partial\bbeta=-2\bX^\top\bY+2\bX^\top\bX\bbeta+\bA^\top\blam=\bzero$ gives $\bhat_H=\bhat-\tfrac12(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bA^\top\hat\blam_H$ $(*)$. Apply $\bA$ and impose $\bA\bhat_H=\bc$: $-\tfrac12\hat\blam_H=[\bA(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bA^\top]^{-1}(\bc-\bA\bhat)$. Substitute.

Why it is the minimum (lecture)

From $(*)$, $2\bX^\top\bX(\bhat-\bhat_H)=\bA^\top\hat\blam_H$. For $\bbeta$ with $\bA\bbeta=\bc$:

$\|\bX\bhat-\bX\bbeta\|^2=\|\bX(\bhat-\bhat_H)\|^2+\|\bX(\bhat_H-\bbeta)\|^2+\hat\blam_H^\top\bA(\bhat_H-\bbeta)$, and the last term is $0$ since $\bA\bhat_H=\bA\bbeta=\bc$. Hence $\beps^\top\beps=\|\bY-\bX\bhat\|^2+\|\bX(\bhat-\bhat_H)\|^2+\|\bX(\bhat_H-\bbeta)\|^2$, minimized iff $\bbeta=\bhat_H$ (full rank).

Useful identities Lecture 11

  2. $\|\bX\bhat-\bX\bbeta\|^2=\|\bX\bhat-\bX\bhat_H\|^2+\|\bX\bhat_H-\bX\bbeta\|^2$ for $\bA\bbeta=\bc$.
  3. $\|\bY-\bX\bbeta\|^2=\|\bX\bbeta-\hat\bY_H\|^2+\|\bY-\hat\bY_H\|^2$ for $\bA\bbeta=\bc$.

(i): cross term $(\bY-\hat\bY)^\top(\hat\bY-\hat\bY_H)=\bY^\top(\bI-\bP)\bX(\bhat-\bhat_H)=0$. (iii): cross term $2(\bX^\top\bY-\bX^\top\bX\bhat_H)^\top(\bhat_H-\bbeta)=\hat\blam_H^\top\bA(\bhat_H-\bbeta)=0$.

Pick $\bbeta_0$ with $\bA\bbeta_0=\bc$, set $\bgam=\bbeta-\bbeta_0$ and $\tilde\bY=\bY-\bX\bbeta_0=\bX\bgam+\beps$. By B.3.3, $W^\perp\cap\Omega=\mathcal C(\bP_\Omega\bM^\top)=\mathcal C(\bX(\bX^\top\bX)^{-1}\bA^\top)$, rank $q$, so

51.9L12Generalized least squares

GLS estimator

$\Var\beps=\sigma^2\bV$, $\bV$ PD, $\bV=\bK\bK^\top$ with $\bK$ nonsingular. Set $\bZ=\bK^{-1}\bY$, $\bB=\bK^{-1}\bX$, $\boldsymbol\eta=\bK^{-1}\beps$, so $\bZ=\bB\bbeta+\boldsymbol\eta$ with $\Var\boldsymbol\eta=\sigma^2\bI$. OLS on the transformed model:

$$\bbeta^*=(\bB^\top\bB)^{-1}\bB^\top\bZ=(\bX^\top\bV^{-1}\bX)^{-1}\bX^\top\bV^{-1}\bY,\quad \E\bbeta^*=\bbeta,\quad \Var\bbeta^*=\sigma^2(\bX^\top\bV^{-1}\bX)^{-1}.$$

$\bbeta^*$ minimizes $(\bY-\bX\bbeta)^\top\bV^{-1}(\bY-\bX\bbeta)$; $\ \mathrm{RSS}^*=(\bY-\bX\bbeta^*)^\top\bV^{-1}(\bY-\bX\bbeta^*)$.

Properties
  • If $\bc^\top\bbeta$ is estimable, $\bc^\top\bbeta^*$ is its unique BLUE (Gauss–Markov on the transformed model).
  • OLS $\bc^\top\bhat$ is still unbiased but $\Var(\bc^\top\bhat)\ge\Var(\bc^\top\bbeta^*)$.
  • Normal errors: $\bbeta^*_{\rm MLE}=\bbeta^*$, $\hat\sigma^{2*}_{\rm MLE}=\mathrm{RSS}^*/n$.
  • Weighted LS: $\bV=\mathrm{diag}(v_i)$ minimizes $\sum\varepsilon_i^2/v_i$. For a straight line with $w_i=1/\sigma_i^2$: $\beta_1^*=\dfrac{\sum w_i(x_i-\bar x_w)(Y_i-\bar Y_w)}{\sum w_i(x_i-\bar x_w)^2}$, $\beta_0^*=\bar Y_w-\beta_1^*\bar x_w$, with weighted means $\bar x_w,\bar Y_w$.
